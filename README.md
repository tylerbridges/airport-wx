# Airports

A static page showing weather and delay risk at U.S. airline-service airports with weather reporting (588 monitored today): big airport codes, colored status pills and a rolling 36-hour risk timeline per airport, with a detail sheet in plain English ("Delays & closures", "FAA traffic notices" incl. the FAA Command Center operations plan, "Weather warnings", "Thunderstorms", "Storm outlook", "Storm forecast") and an Aviation-mode "Pilot details" section holding the coded material (decoded and raw METAR, raw TAF, LAMP table, SIGMET and CWA text, TCF tops). Risk reasons stay coded in the data (and history); the page maps them to traveler wording ("Ceiling 400 ft" → "Very low clouds", "Gusts 30 kt" → "Wind gusts to 35 mph", "GDP avg 49m" → "Arrival delays ~49 min"). Sections with nothing in them are hidden; a line at the bottom says which sources were checked, or which failed. Dark-first, inspired by iOS design.

Every poll's predictions and observed outcomes are also recorded to the `history` branch (see "History") so a later phase can verify and train a model.

## Product goal

A user should feel confident they're aware of any potential disruption, weather or not. Clean by default (traveler language), detail behind toggles (aviation mode), and never silently wrong (a source failure or stale data is always stated).

## Architecture

aviationweather.gov does not send CORS headers, so a browser can't call it. Instead:

1. A GitHub Actions workflow (`.github/workflows/poll.yml`) runs every 10 minutes (and on push / manual dispatch).
2. `poller/poll.mjs` fetches every source server-side (each independent, 20 s timeout, per-source `{ok, at, error}`), scores risk with `poller/risk.mjs`, and writes `site/data/status.json`.
3. The workflow uploads `site/` as a Pages artifact and deploys it. Nothing is committed to `main`.
4. After the deploy, `poller/record.mjs` appends to a checkout of the `history` branch and `poller/history.sh` commits and pushes it (see "History"). Those steps are `continue-on-error`, run after the deploy, and can't fail the job.
5. The page (`site/index.html`, `site/app.js`) only reads `./data/status.json` (re-fetched every 2 minutes and when the tab becomes visible). If it 404s it falls back to the committed `site/data/sample.json` and shows a "Sample data" banner.

The Live ticker dispatches a poll every five minutes. Before dispatching, `tools/recover-poll.mjs` cancels main-branch polls waiting at an unprotected `github-pages` environment gate for at least 20 minutes, freeing the Pages concurrency slot. Gates with reviewers, a wait timer, another environment or unknown reviewer metadata are left alone. Active poll jobs also have a 20-minute timeout.

The poller exits 0 unless every source failed, so a partial outage still deploys; the page lists failed sources ("FAA delay info unavailable").

A Cloudflare Worker ("Live relay" below) can add fresher data on top: when `data/config.json` names it, the page asks it for its starred and visible airports on every open, refresh and every 2 minutes.

## Data sources

- METAR: https://aviationweather.gov/api/data/metar (plus one `&hours=24` request for the major airports: the timeline's observed past hours, `observed[]`; not a status source, so if it fails `observed` is simply left out and those hours show grey)
- TAF: https://aviationweather.gov/api/data/taf
- Convective SIGMETs: https://aviationweather.gov/api/data/airsigmet (point-in-polygon, currently valid CONVECTIVE only)
- Additional operational SIGMETs: https://aviationweather.gov/api/data/isigmet?format=json. Severe turbulence (`TURB` + `SEV`), severe icing (`ICE` + `SEV`) and volcanic ash (`VA`), with `validTimeFrom/To`, `base/top` in feet, AREA polygon coordinates, and raw text. Also accepts these hazards from the existing domestic SIGMET feed. Free public NOAA AWC API; no key, one shared fetch per poll.
- FAA NAS status (ground stops, ground delay programs, delays, closures): https://nasstatus.faa.gov/api/airport-status-information. Closure reasons are NOTAM text; `poller/notam.mjs` reads the scope (full airport / particular runways / limited to some users such as GA), the effective times (`YYMMDDHHMM-YYMMDDHHMM`, `PERM`, `EST`; preferred over the Reopen field) and writes a plain-English summary.
- FAA ATCSCC: https://www.fly.faa.gov/adv/advADB.jsp is "The Most Recent ATCSCC Advisory" (a single advisory, not a list). Usually that is the Command Center's **operations plan** ("ATCSCC ADVZY 072 DCC 10/03/2026 OPERATIONS PLAN", body after "RAW TEXT:" in a `<pre>`), parsed by `poller/opsplan.mjs` (see "FAA operations plan"). When it is a program advisory (ground stop, GDP, AFP with "IMPACTING CONDITION"), it is parsed as before. Its only link is the "Advisories Database Selection Form" (`/adv/advAdvisoryForm`), whose form submits `GET /adv/adv_list?whichAdvisories=ATCSCC&advisoryCategory=NotAll&date=YYYY-MM-DD&gStop=true&gDelay=true&airflow=true…` (`sources.mjs` `atcsccListUrl`): the advisory list for that date, one table per category (Airspace Flow Programs, Ground Stops, Ground Delay Programs) with rows number (link to `/adv/adv_otherdis?adv_date=MMDDYYYY&advn=N`), control element, date, brief title, send time, or "NO ADVISORIES MATCH YOUR SELECTION CRITERIA!". The poller reads today's and yesterday's (UTC) lists and follows the newest program advisory per control element and program (not "PROPOSED" ones; up to 40, 4 at a time, 45 s budget). Per control element and program type the latest advisory decides; it is active when it isn't a cancellation (CNX), its period has started and its end is after now. When nothing could be parsed, nothing was followable and no list page was recognised, the source is **degraded**: `ok` with an `error` ("advisory list unavailable …"), so it never counts as fresh (health and the checked line say "FAA traffic notices partly unavailable"). A recognised list saying "no advisories match" is a real, quiet answer. Fixtures: `atcscc-list-real.html` / `atcscc-detail-real.html` (real Oct 5 captures), `atcscc-list.html` (quiet list, also in `scenarios/_base/`).
- NWS alerts: https://api.weather.gov/alerts/active, one national snapshot matched to each airport using warning polygons or affected-zone polygons. Zone geometries are cached for 30 days; missing or failed geographic coverage marks the NWS source unavailable, never silently empty. The live relay continues point queries for its at-most-12 requested airports.
- SPC Day 1 categorical outlook: https://www.spc.noaa.gov/products/outlook/day1otlk_cat.nolyr.geojson
- NWS LAMP text bulletin (hourly guidance): `https://nomads.ncep.noaa.gov/pub/data/nccf/com/lmp/prod/lmp.YYYYMMDD/lmp.tHH30z.lavtxt.ascii`, newest HH:30 cycle, stepping back up to 3 hours on 404. Real CONUS blocks have hourly P01/PC1/LP1/LC1/CP1/CC1 rows plus PPO, PCO, P06, POZ, POS, TYP, CLD, CIG, CCG, VIS, CVS, OBV, WGS; there is no LP2/CP2 row, and some blocks (e.g. Hawaii) have no probability rows at all. Per airport and hour: WGS gust (kt, "NG" = 0), `tstmProb` = LP1 (1-hour lightning probability for the hour ending at that column; LP2, 2-hour, only if LP1 is missing), `convProb` = CP1 (1-hour convection probability; CP2 fallback), `probHrs` (1 or 2), CIG and VIS categories, TYP, POZ, PPO. Columns are found by character position under the UTC row (right-justified 3-character fields), so sparse rows such as P06 (a value every 6th column) line up. Missing values are runs of 9s ("99", "999", "22999999…" in a real PHJH block): a "999" cell is null in every row, and each row has a valid range (gust 0–98 kt, so "99" is missing; CIG 1–8; VIS 1–7; probabilities 0–100), so a sentinel never becomes a 999-kt gust.
- AWC TFM Convective Forecast: https://aviationweather.gov/api/data/tcf?format=geojson (Mar–Oct; empty/204 is normal). Live properties: `validTime`/`issueTime` as "YYYYMMDD_HHMM" (UTC), `coverage` ("sparse" → low; "medium"; "high"/"solid" → high), `confidence`, `tops` ("390", ">400"). Areas containing the airport (or within 10 NM of the edge), with valid time, coverage, confidence and tops.
- AWC Center Weather Advisories: https://aviationweather.gov/api/data/cwa?format=json. Live records: `hazard` ("TS"), `qualifier`, `validTimeFrom`/`validTimeTo` (epoch seconds), `coords` [{lat, lon} as strings], `rawText`. Advisories whose polygon contains the airport or passes within 10 NM of it.

- Nearby FAA Temporary Flight Restrictions from tfr.faa.gov (see "Flight restrictions").
- Aircraft movement (ADS-B, see "Movement"): [adsb.fi](https://adsb.fi) open data (primary), [ADSB.lol](https://adsb.lol) (fallback; data © ADSB.lol contributors, [ODbL 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/)).

Requests send `User-Agent: airport-wx (github.com/tylerbridges/airport-wx)`, time out after 20 s, and fail independently; a failed source is listed in `sources` and never stops the poll.

LAMP, ATCSCC, TCF and CWA were first written from documentation; on Oct 3 2026 they were checked against real responses from `raw/latest/` on the `history` branch (LAMP: LP1/CP1 instead of LP2; ATCSCC: the page is the operations plan; TCF: "YYYYMMDD_HHMM" times; CWA matched). The fixtures `lamp.txt`, `atcscc.html`, `tcf.json` and `cwa.json` are those real formats, with only their times turned into template tokens. Parsers return empty results on anything unexpected.

Convective SIGMETs, CWAs and TCF areas count an airport as affected when it is inside the polygon, on its edge, or within 10 NM of it (`NEAR_NM` in `poller/lib.mjs`): SIGMET 80E on Oct 3 2026 ("FROM 30NNW CLT-20N CLT-CLT-30NNW SPA") had CLT as a vertex and plain ray casting missed it.

### FAA operations plan

`poller/opsplan.mjs` reads the DCC operations plan: advisory number, date, issue time (signature line), EVENT TIME, the narrative paragraph between the rules of underscores, and these sections (headers are lines ending in ":"):

- STAFFING TRIGGER(S): "UNTIL 0100 -BNA OPERATIONS" → {facility, detail, until}. "UNTIL hhmm" (UTC) rolls past midnight relative to the issue time.
- TERMINAL CONSTRAINTS: "CLT/ATL/MCO/TPA/IAH/HOU - VCTS", "N90 - WIND" → per airport {reason}.
- TERMINAL ACTIVE / PLANNED: "UNTIL 0059 -SAN GROUND DELAY PROGRAM", "UNTIL 2300 -MCO/TPA GROUND STOP POSSIBLE", "… GROUND STOP/DELAY PROGRAM POSSIBLE" → {airports, program: GS|GDP|GS/GDP, status: active|possible, until}.
- RUNWAY/EQUIPMENT/…(SIRs): "DEN - RWY 16R/34L CLOSED UNTIL 11/05/2026 0000Z" (2- or 4-digit years) → {airport, item, status: closed|limited|out of service|construction|maintenance, runways, until}.
- EN ROUTE CONSTRAINTS/ACTIVE/PLANNED, CDRS/SWAP/…, AIRSPACE FLOW PROGRAM(S) ACTIVE/PLANNED, PLANNED LAUNCH/REENTRY (name, site, primary and backup windows): national lists.
- Narrative sentences with DELAY/DEVIATION name airports ("ZJX REPORTS THAT TPA AND MCO ARE STILL EXPERIENCING SOME DEVIATIONS …, AND DELAYS WILL CONTINUE").

Facility → airports (`FACILITY_AIRPORTS`): N90 → JFK/LGA/EWR; A80 → ATL; C90 → ORD/MDW; NCT → SFO/OAK/SJC; SCT → LAX/SAN/SNA/BUR/ONT/LGB; D10 → DFW/DAL; I90 → IAH/HOU; PCT → DCA/IAD/BWI; A90 → BOS; D01 → DEN; L30 → LAS; P50 → PHX; M98 → MSP; S46 → SEA; F11 → MCO; A11 → ANC; HCF → HNL; in staffing triggers MIA (TRACON) → MIA/FLL. Any other 3-letter code is the airport itself (BNA, PHL, CLT). Centers (ZNY, ZJX, ZOA …) map to no airport and go to the national `opsplan` (for a future national summary).

### Program causes

FAA NAS status and ATCSCC programs get a cause class from their reason text (`poller/cause.mjs`): weather, volume, equipment (outages, radar/ILS, frequencies), staffing (incl. ATC zero), runway (construction, closures, configuration), security, airline (company/airline request, IT outage), vip (VIP movement, TFR), space (launch/reentry), other, unknown. The category before the first "/" or ":" wins, else keywords. Programs score by program type alone; the cause only names the reason, e.g. "Ground stop — air traffic control staffing, until 7:30 PM ET" or "Ground stop — airline request (IT outage)". A leading category header ("WEATHER / THUNDERSTORMS", "RWY-TAXI / CONSTRUCTION", "WX:Low Ceilings") names the class and only the detail after it is shown; otherwise a slash joins details and every part is kept ("snow/ice" → "weather (snow and ice)", "tstms/wind" → "weather (thunderstorms and wind)"), with FAA shorthand spelled out ("wind/rwy config" → "wind and runway configuration"). FAA clock times with a stated zone ("7:45 pm EDT" for SFO; EST/EDT/CST/CDT/MST/MDT/PST/PDT/AKST/AKDT/HST, UTC/Z, or ET/CT/MT/PT) are read in that zone (`lib.mjs` `faaTimeMs`), else in the airport's, and are written in the airport's local time ("4:45 PM PT"); the page's `retime` reads a written zone the same way. When the ops plan lists an active program that the NAS status or an active advisory already has, the plan item takes that cause ("Ground delay program until 1:59 AM — runway work or configuration (construction)"), and the plan's terminal constraint ("wind") is kept only as `constraint`, shown in More details → FAA plan.

## Risk levels

FAA narrative statements about reduced, ended or cancelled delays and no issues do not score unless continuing or residual disruption is also stated. Possible holding is described as possible delays, never confirmed delays. Permanent VIP protection restrictions are informational; temporary movement windows still score.

0 None (green "Clear"), 1 Low (teal "Minor"), 2 Moderate (amber), 3 High (orange-red), 4 Severe (purple). The highest matching rule wins. Program rules apply whatever the cause.

- **Severe (4):** FAA ground stop or full airport closure (`AD AP CLSD` with no "TO …"/"EXC"/"PPR" qualifier, or one whose only exemptions are emergency, medevac/lifeguard, relief, military, evacuation or PPR flights, e.g. "AD AP CLSD EXC MEDEVAC AND EMERG ACFT": "Airport closed except emergency and medical flights."; all runways closed, or a non-NOTAM closure; from the NAS status); active ATCSCC ground stop (advisory or ops plan "TERMINAL ACTIVE") not already in the NAS status; thunderstorm with `+` or gusts >= 45 kt; freezing rain; SPC MDT/HIGH; NWS Tornado, Blizzard, Ice Storm, Hurricane, Extreme Wind warnings.
- **High (3):** ground delay program (NAS status, or an active ATCSCC advisory / ops plan GDP not already listed there); TS or VCTS; LIFR (ceiling BKN/OVC/VV < 500 ft or visibility < 1 sm); gusts >= 35 kt; `+SN`, or SN with visibility <= 1/2 sm; FZDZ or PL; SPC ENH; LAMP thunder chance (LP1) >= 40% ("Thunder chance 45% (LAMP)"); TCF high coverage over the airport; Severe Thunderstorm, Winter Storm, Tropical Storm, High Wind warnings; convective SIGMET over (or within 10 NM of) the airport.
- **Moderate (2):** any general arrival/departure delay; IFR (ceiling < 1000 ft or visibility < 3 sm); gusts >= 25 kt; SN; SPC SLGT; LAMP thunder chance (LP1) 20–39%; LAMP convection (CP1) >= 50% when the thunder chance is lower ("Storms likely nearby (LAMP)"); TCF medium coverage; Center Weather Advisory for thunderstorms/convection or IFR over the airport; Winter Weather, Wind, Dense Fog advisories; ops plan "GROUND STOP POSSIBLE" / "DELAY PROGRAM POSSIBLE" until its time ("FAA plans a possible ground stop until 7 PM (storms)", cause from the airport's terminal constraint, else "conditions"); ops plan staffing trigger at the airport or its TRACON until its time ("Air traffic control staffing shortage until 8 PM — delays possible"); ops plan narrative naming the airport with delays/deviations, until the plan's valid end ("FAA reports delays at MCO/TPA expected to continue"). A VIP TFR within 30 NM while it is active ("VIP movement — brief ground holds possible 2–4 PM"). See "Flight restrictions".
- **Related-airport spillover:** research-only; never raises live risk.
- **Low (1):** MVFR; RA/DZ/BR; SPC MRGL; a single-runway closure ("Runway 7L/25R closed"); an ops plan terminal constraint with no possible/active program ("FAA reports nearby storms affecting arrivals"); an ops plan SIR runway closure or construction ("Runway 16R/34L closed until Nov 4", the airport's local date; never above Low, and not repeated when the NAS status already lists that runway); a SIR glideslope/ILS outage or limited operations, only in hours whose flight category is IFR or LIFR. a space-operations TFR within 30 NM ("Space launch nearby — airspace restrictions 5–7 PM").
- **Informational (0, available through Airport details, never sets a level):** SPC TSTM ("General thunderstorms possible in the area (no severe risk)"); closures limited to some users — only when "CLSD TO …" names a narrowing subset (GA, transient, private, non-scheduled, a size/weight limit, particular runways) or the "EXC …" lets scheduled airline flights operate (e.g. "CLSD TO NON SKED TRANSIENT GA … EXC 24HR PPR": "Closed to private (non-scheduled, general aviation) flights unless approved 24 hours ahead. Airline flights aren't affected."; size-limited "CLSD TO NON SKED ACFT WINGSPAN MORE THAN 214FT …": "Closed to very large non-scheduled aircraft (747-8/A380 size). Airline flights aren't affected."; "general aviation" only when GA/TRANSIENT/PRIVATE is in the text); closures that haven't started or have ended; SIR glideslope/limited-ops in VFR/MVFR hours; taxiway SIRs; an ops plan program already in the NAS status (NAS wins; if NAS gives no end, the plan's end is used). security/stadium/hazard/air-show TFRs (the Flight restrictions popup, never a reason).

Hourly (24 rows from the top of the current hour): the prevailing TAF state per hour from the base group plus FM/BECMG groups (BECMG takes effect at `timeBec` if present, else `timeFrom`); TEMPO groups count at full level; PROB groups count one level lower with "Chance of" in the reason. Hour 0 takes the METAR (the observation wins: TAF conditions are left out of hour 0 when there is a current METAR) and SIGMETs. FAA programs (ground stop, GDP, delays) and active ATCSCC GS/GDP score every hour until their stated end; with no end they hold 3 hours (5 when the delays are increasing) and say "until further notice". A full airport closure scores every hour of its effective window (`risk.mjs` `closureSpan`): from its start (NOTAM start, else already in effect) through its reopening (NOTAM end, else the FAA's Reopen time), as "Airport closed until 11 PM ET" ("until Fri 6 AM ET" past midnight, "until Nov 4" beyond a day; with a cause "Airport closed — weather (snow removal), until 9 PM ET"); a closure that starts later in the 24 hours scores from its start; a permanent one every hour; one with no known end hour 0 only. Runway and SIR runway closures score hour 0. NWS alerts and CWAs apply to hours between their start and end; the SPC category applies to hours until 12Z next day; each LAMP LP1/CP1 value applies to the hour ending at its column time (LP2/CP2: 2 hours); a TCF area applies within an hour of its valid time. Ranges are written "4–7 PM" only within one day and one AM/PM half, else "11 AM – 2 PM" or "11 PM – 1 AM tomorrow". `now` = hour 0; `peak` = max over 24 hours (earliest hour of that max). Reasons in a box are deduped, with one Visibility/Ceiling/Gusts item each (the highest; the observed one on a tie). Airports are sorted by peak, then now, then IATA code.

The sheet shows one box ("Minor · through 3 AM") while the level holds; a separate "Peak 4–7 PM" box only when the peak is later and higher than now. The card's line uses the same rule.

## Hub cascade — research only

Related-airport spillover is parked. It never raises live airport risk, creates a public cascade note,
changes delay probabilities or adds a knock-on trip concern. Directly applicable FAA restrictions remain active.

`core.mjs` keeps `hubResearch` version 1: the approximate top-route candidates, missing/available hub
coverage, weather issue timestamps, the hypothetical 1–4-hour exposure window, signals and explicit
zero-signal controls. `record.mjs` archives these every poll in truth and hourly in forecast; current-hour
hub states are deduplicated in truth and baseline delay estimates and model metadata are retained in forecast. FAA program
causes, scope, start/end when supplied, trend, advisories and source failures remain recorded. These
as-issued research inputs can later be joined to FAA/ADS-B/BTS outcomes without claiming causation.
They are not a deployed model feature. Existing trained hub-weather inputs are separate and unchanged.
See ROADMAP.md for validation and promotion requirements. The historical cascade helpers are retained
for offline experimentation; `applyCascade` is not called by live assembly.

## Additional flight weather advisories

`poller/aviation-advisories.mjs` interprets severe turbulence, severe icing and volcanic ash SIGMETs, including international advisories from the AWC. AREA polygons within 10 NM of the airport are matched with date-line handling. Invalid geometry/time/altitude ranges, expired advisories, moderate turbulence/icing, and starts beyond the next 24 hours are omitted. Missing altitude stays explicitly unknown. The parser is checked against real NOAA responses retrieved October 4, 2026 (`sigmet-advisories-real.json`). Unsupported line/circle geometry is not interpreted; this is additional coverage, not comprehensive flight-route monitoring.

The airport detail sheet shows a concise **Flight weather** card only when relevant, with altitude and local valid times; raw text is available in Aviation mode. Wind/winter toggles apply to turbulence/icing; volcanic ash remains visible. Trip endpoint notes require an exact overlap with the scheduled departure or arrival and retain the altitude. The actual flight route and altitude are unknown, so these informational advisories do not alter risk levels, delay probabilities, trip status, or imply an airport closure. The delay model needs no retraining for this addition.

The live relay carries these advisories from the latest build and drops expired entries; their source freshness/availability remains labeled as build data. They are also retained in the bounded offline snapshot. Scenarios: `turbulence-advisory`, `icing-advisory`, `volcanic-ash-advisory`.

## Flight restrictions

FAA Temporary Flight Restrictions are fetched from `https://tfr.faa.gov/tfrapi/exportTfrList` and XML details at `https://tfr.faa.gov/download/detail_<id>.xml`, without an API key. The list is refreshed each run; up to 60 relevant details are cached for one hour, with a three-hour fallback on detail failure. The source reports availability separately in `status.noticeSources.tfr`.

`poller/tfr.mjs` parses areas and schedules; `poller/notices.mjs` includes restrictions within 30 NM during the next 24 hours. Active VIP restrictions are Moderate (brief ground holds possible); space operations are Low (rerouting possible). Security, stadium, hazard and air-show restrictions are information only. Long-standing information-only restrictions are omitted. The app displays **Flight restrictions**, with raw text/distance in Aviation mode and specific availability wording on a source failure. Tests: `poller/notices.test.mjs`, `poller/tfr.test.mjs`; scenario: `vip-tfr`.

Airport NOTAM fetching, credentials, cache, parsing, scoring and monitoring were removed. The app does not claim comprehensive runway, taxiway or ILS outage coverage. FAA NAS closure parsing (`poller/notam.mjs`), ground stops, delay programs and operations-plan constraints remain supported independently.

## The page (build 2b)

- **Settings** live in `site/prefs.js` (classic script in `<head>`, `window.AWXPrefs`; `require()`-able in Node): `getPrefs()`, `setPref(key, value)`, `onPrefs(fn)` (returns an unsubscribe; also fires for changes made in another tab), `DEFAULTS`, `CATEGORIES`. ## Live relay

The Actions build runs every ~5 minutes and the deploy adds a few more, so the page's data is usually 5–15 minutes old. The live relay is a Cloudflare Worker (`worker/worker.mjs`, script name `airport-wx-live`) that answers with data at most about a minute old for the airports the page shows.

- **Endpoints.** `GET /health` → `{ok, version, time}`. `GET /status?ids=ORD,MSP,KFCM` (IATA or ICAO, up to 12; optional `&tz=KFCM:America/Chicago` for airports outside the curated list) → `{live: true, generated, build: {ok, generated}, ids, unknown, sources, airports, wx, h0}`: `airports` are curated airports in exactly the status.json shape (fields the relay doesn't compute carry over from the build), `wx` holds other airports by ICAO in the `data/wx/<letter>.json` shard-entry shape (3-letter codes outside the curated list go to `unknown`), `sources` has every status.json source with `{ok, at, error}` plus `live: true` (fetched by the relay), `from: "build"`, or `stale: true` + `liveError` (the live request failed and the build's value is used).
- **What is live.** Per request: AWC METAR and TAF for the requested ids only, AWC airsigmet, FAA NAS status, and NWS alerts per point for the requested curated airports, each with `User-Agent: airport-wx (github.com/tylerbridges/airport-wx)`. LAMP (~4.5 MB), SPC, TCF, CWA and ATCSCC advisories come from the latest build (`https://tylerbridges.github.io/airport-wx/data/status.json`). Levels are recomputed with the poller's own functions (`poller/core.mjs` → `risk.mjs`), so with the same inputs the result is identical to the build (a test checks this).
- **Failures.** Every source is independent. A failed live source falls back to the build's value and is marked `stale`; for airports outside the curated list the build's shard (`data/wx/<letter>.json`) is fetched only when the live METAR or TAF request failed. 502 only when the build and every live source failed.
- **Caching.** Each upstream response is kept 60 s (AWC asks for at most one request per minute per thread), status.json and shards 120 s, and the finished reply 30 s keyed by the sorted ids. Two layers: memory in the Worker isolate, then the Cache API. On `*.workers.dev` the Cache API does nothing (it needs a custom domain), so there it's per isolate: with many users on many isolates AWC can see more than one request a minute.
- **CORS.** `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` only for `https://tylerbridges.github.io` and `http://localhost:*`; GET and OPTIONS.
- **Limits.** Free plan: 100,000 requests a day, 10 ms CPU per request, 50 subrequests per request (a 12-airport request makes at most ~19: METAR, TAF, airsigmet, FAA, 12 NWS points, status.json, shards). Measured in Node 22 on the fixtures (`node worker/dev.mjs bench`, medians, stub cost subtracted): with every cache empty ~4.5 ms for 4 airports and ~8 ms for 12 (most of it the shared risk functions, ~0.3 ms per airport, and parsing the ~220 KB status.json); with the upstreams cached ~1.6 / ~4.6 ms; a cached reply ~0.3 / ~0.7 ms. The 90th percentile of a 12-airport request with empty caches is over 10 ms (garbage collection), and the first request in a new isolate also compiles the modules, so the free plan's limit can occasionally be hit; the paid plan (30 s CPU) removes that.
- **Page.** `site/data/config.json` = `{"liveUrl": "https://airport-wx-live.<subdomain>.workers.dev"}`, written by the "Live relay URL for the page" step of `poll.yml` once the relay's `/health` is ok (committed default `{"liveUrl": null}`: no relay). `site/app.js` (`// live relay`) asks for starred airports first (non-curated ones through `site/searched.js`), then the visible list, at most 12; merges them over the build; shows "Live · 40 s ago", or "Live updates unavailable — showing the latest build" (amber; its age added once it is over 20 min old) when the call fails, and then shows the build only. The refresh button waits for the relay. Test scenarios and sample data never call it. The check page has a "Live relay" row: `/health` ok and `/status?ids=MSP` with a METAR fetched under 3 minutes ago; with no liveUrl it's a warning ("not configured").
- **Deploy.** `.github/workflows/worker.yml` runs on pushes to main that touch `worker/**`, `poller/**` or `airports.json`, and on manual dispatch. Without the secrets it prints "Cloudflare secrets not set; skipping live relay deploy" and succeeds. With them: unit tests, `node worker/pack.mjs dist`, `PUT /accounts/$ID/workers/scripts/airport-wx-live` (multipart: `metadata` = `{main_module: "worker.mjs", compatibility_date: "2026-09-01", bindings}` plus one `application/javascript+module` part per module), `POST …/scripts/airport-wx-live/subdomain {"enabled": true}`, `GET …/workers/subdomain`, then `/health` and `/status?ids=MSP,ORD` must answer (retried for ~2 minutes); the URL goes to the job summary. No wrangler or npm.
- **Module layout.** Uploaded flat: `worker/worker.mjs` → `worker.mjs` (main module) and `poller/x.mjs` → `x.mjs`; `worker/pack.mjs` follows the imports and rewrites the worker's `../poller/x.mjs` specifiers to `./x.mjs` (the poller modules already import each other as `./x.mjs`). It refuses `node:` or bare imports and name collisions, so any module the worker imports must stay pure. Bindings: `VERSION` (commit) and `AIRPORTS` (`airports.json`, so curated airports resolve even when the build can't be fetched).
- **Setup.** In Cloudflare: open Workers & Pages once so the account has a `workers.dev` subdomain; create an API token (My Profile → API Tokens → Create Custom Token) with **Account → Workers Scripts → Edit** for this account; copy the Account ID (Workers & Pages overview). In GitHub: Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions → add `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` and `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID`. Then run "Live relay deploy" (Actions → Run workflow); the next "Poll and deploy" writes the URL into `data/config.json`.
- **Rotating the token.** Create the new token with the same permission, replace the `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` secret, run "Live relay deploy" once to confirm it works, then delete the old token in Cloudflare. The token is only used by the deploy job and the poll workflow's config step; the running Worker doesn't use it.
- **Locally.** `node worker/dev.mjs serve` runs the handler on http://localhost:8787 with every upstream answered from `poller/fixtures`, and serves `site/` on http://localhost:8000 pointed at it. Tests: `worker/worker.test.mjs` (in `node poller/run-tests.mjs`).

## Trips

Personal flight features are **off by default**, including for existing installations. Enable them in **Settings → Flights → Enable flight features** to add Trips, calendar connections/imports, a departure-day flight brief and itinerary airport highlights/routes. `site/flight-features.js` downloads the trip module only after opt-in. Disabling hides every personal flight entry point, cancels calendar requests and stops refreshes; saved trips and connections remain on the device and return when reenabled. Airports and Map work without opting in. The preference (`flights`, a boolean in `awx-settings`) persists on this device. Server-side polling of an administrator-configured calendar is independent of this device preference.

People who opt in and connect their flight calendar (or add a trip by hand) see the things that may affect it in the Trips tab, in a compact departure-day flight brief, and as a "Your flight" row in each relevant airport's sheet.

**Source.** The flight-tracking app syncs flights into an iPhone calendar; that iCloud calendar is shared as a public calendar, and its link (`webcal://p…-caldav.icloud.com/published/2/…`, fetched as `https://`) is the GitHub Actions secret `FLIGHTY_ICS_URL`. `poll.yml` passes it to the poll step; `worker.yml` adds it to the Worker as a `secret_text` binding of the same name when the secret exists (the Worker doesn't use it yet). Manual trips are stored only on the device (`localStorage` `awx-trips`) and merged with calendar trips in the page.

**Privacy.** The site is public, so `data/trips.json` holds only airports and times: `{generated, configured, ok, error, source, count, flights, trips: [{id, legs: [{from, to, dep, arr}]}]}` (IATA codes, ISO UTC times; `id` = SHA-256 of the event UID salted with the secret link, not reversible). Flight numbers, names, confirmation codes, seats and notes are read only to recognise flights and are never stored or published. The link is never logged or written to a file: fetch errors are generic ("HTTP 404 from the calendar", "timeout after 20s"), any text containing it is masked, and in Actions its `https://` form and path are masked too (`::add-mask::`). The check page fails if `trips.json` has any key, value or pattern beyond codes, times and hashed ids (flight-number pattern, email, free text).

**Pipeline** (`poller/trips-poll.mjs`, three `// trips hook` lines in `poll.mjs`):

1. Each run fetches the calendar once (20 s timeout). No secret: `trips.json` = `{configured: false, trips: []}`. A failed fetch: `{configured: true, ok: false, error}` — the page says the calendar couldn't be read.
2. `poller/trips.mjs` (pure, RFC 5545) unfolds lines and reads VEVENTs (VALARM/VTIMEZONE ignored): DTSTART/DTEND with TZID (Mozilla-style prefixes stripped), `Z` or floating (the airport's own zone, then `X-WR-TIMEZONE`), DURATION when there is no DTEND, VALUE=DATE (all-day: ignored), STATUS:CANCELLED or a "Canceled:" summary (ignored), RRULE (only the first instance), RECURRENCE-ID overrides (skipped).
3. Flight detection is tolerant because the app's exact event format is unverified: a pair of airport codes joined by → -> - – — "to" or ✈ in SUMMARY, LOCATION or DESCRIPTION ("✈ DL 1234 · MSP → ATL", "DL1234 MSP-ATL"); otherwise a flight marker ("Flight", ✈ or a flight number like "DL 1234") plus the departure airport from LOCATION (a code such as "(MSP)", else an airport name or city) and the destination from "to <city or code>" in SUMMARY ("Flight to Atlanta (DL1234)"), "from X to Y", another known code, or the rest of the summary as a place. Codes must be in the airport list (`airports.json` + `site/data/airports-all.json`, so "THE" or "ABC" in notes don't count); a city maps to its main airport (curated first, then scheduled, larger first: "Chicago" → ORD).
4. Departure = DTSTART, arrival = DTEND, each in its own TZID. Consecutive flights where the arrival airport is the next departure airport and the gap is under 8 h form one trip (connections). Trips are kept when the first departure is at most 7 days ahead and the last scheduled arrival at most 24 h ago. This is a retention window, not confirmed flight progress.
5. Every trip airport (origin, connections, destination) joins this run's full pipeline even when it isn't one of the curated majors: it is looked up in `airports-all.json` (+ `airports-extra.json`) for ICAO, lat/lon and zone, fetched with FAA, NWS, SPC, LAMP, METAR/TAF like a major, and marked `trip: true` in status.json (the page keeps those off its At risk list unless starred; the check page reports their METARs under "Trips").
6. A redacted structural sample goes to the raw folder (`trips-sample.json`, listed as `trips` in `sources.json`, so it lands in `raw/latest/` on the history branch): calendar and event property names with their parameter names, time shapes (`TZID=America/Chicago:########T######`), counts (events, flights, all-day, cancelled, not flights, no end, how each flight was detected) and the first six events' SUMMARY/LOCATION/DESCRIPTION with every digit turned into `#` and every word that isn't an airport code or a format word ("Flight", "to", "Terminal", "Gate", "Seat", "Confirmation" …) turned into `x`s — e.g. `✈ XX #### · MSP → ATL`. Check it with `git show origin/history:raw/latest/trips-sample.json`.

`FIXTURES_DIR`'s `trips.ics` is read in fixture mode (tokens `{{ics+90 America/Chicago}}` = local time 90 min from now, `{{icsz+90}}` UTC, `{{icsd+1}}` a date); `TRIPS_OUT` changes the output path (default: `trips.json` next to the status file being written). `poller/fixtures/trips.ics` holds MSP→ORD (ORD ground stop), DCA→DEN→SLC (45-minute connection in the DEN winter storm), a flight 26 h out, an all-day "Trip to Atlanta", a cancelled flight and a dentist appointment.

**Concerns** (`poller/trip-risk.mjs`, pure; `site/trip-risk.js` is a byte-identical copy — `cp poller/trip-risk.mjs site/trip-risk.js`; a test fails if they differ). Per leg, from the airports' status.json entries:

- Departure airport around the departure time and arrival airport around the arrival time (hours overlapping ±1 h): the weather level and reasons (FAA program reasons are taken out and reported separately; when a program set the hour's level, the weather part is scored from its own reasons), plus the Phase 3 delay chance when `hours[i].delay` exists (`{p, minutes}`; ignored when absent). A connection is one window from the arrival to the next departure.
- FAA programs that hit this flight: a ground delay program or ground stop at the ARRIVAL airport at the departure time holds the flight at its origin ("ORD ground delay program: flights to ORD are held — your 6:05 PM MSP→ORD departure may wait ~45 min."; the wait is the program's average delay); at the departure airport an arrival ground stop is informational; closure, departure delays, an ops-plan possible ground stop or delay program, a staffing trigger or FAA-reported delays covering the departure; at the arrival airport a closure, arrival delays, staffing or FAA-reported delays covering the arrival (and a program there once the flight is airborne: arrivals slowed). A GDP at the departure airport is Low (it holds flights arriving there, not departures).
- Closures hold their whole window: a leg departing from or arriving at a fully closed airport during the closure is Severe (Disruption): "MIA is closed until 6 PM — your 4:15 PM arrival is likely cancelled or diverted." / "… your 3 PM departure is likely delayed or cancelled."
- Related-airport spillover is research-only and creates no trip concern; actual FAA programs at the flight’s airports still apply.
- Connections: tight when under 60 min (90 if any airport on the trip is outside the US); flagged when the connecting airport is Moderate or worse around the arrival or has a delay program (Moderate; High with a GDP or supported likely-delay prediction; Severe with a ground stop or closure). Under 30 min with nothing going on: a Low note.
- Each concern is one plain sentence with a level; they are ordered by severity (programs, then connections, then weather). Trip status: Weather concerns (High/Severe weather without a supported likely-delay prediction), On track (≤ Minor), Possible delays (Moderate), Delays likely (High), Disruption (Severe), Too early to tell (no future flight time inside the 24-hour forecasts yet), Data incomplete (expired past forecast coverage), and Flight status unconfirmed (scheduled departure passed without a material airport concern). Times are schedules: passing departure or arrival never confirms takeoff/landing or removes covered airport concerns. After 24 h beyond the last scheduled arrival, trips leave the active list; manual trips remain editable/deletable in Settings with a neutral Past schedule status. A program first appearing after an expired scheduled time is not backdated to that flight. The top sentence is the most severe concern, or "No weather or FAA issues expected around your flight times."

**Page** (`site/trips.js`, a module; hooks in `app.js` marked `// trips hook`: `render()` → `AWXTrips.render()`, `renderSheet()` → `AWXTrips.decorateSheet(sheet, a)`, `liveQuery()` → trip airports first in the live relay call; `index.html` has `<section id="trips">` and the script tag). The Airports page shows one compact “Today’s flight brief” row only on a scheduled departure day in the origin airport’s local time: route, scheduled time, overall outlook and a reminder that actual flight status is unconfirmed. It opens the full trip sheet. Future trips, full concern text and trip timelines stay in the Trips tab. Every trip card and sheet labels times as scheduled and states that actual flight status is unavailable. Connection minutes and warnings are schedule-based; actual arrivals, gates and walking times are unavailable. The trip sheet: each leg with its departure and arrival airports' level at those times, connection time, airport concerns grouped by itinerary role, buttons to open each airport's sheet, and "From your flight calendar · updated N min ago" (manual trips: "Added by you", Edit and Delete). Airport sheets of trip airports add a “Your flights” board beneath the standard timeline. Each itinerary shows every numbered leg with its route, airport-local scheduled departure/arrival times and dates, a per-leg airport outlook, forecast-availability note and connection minutes/warnings between legs. The board is additive: it never replaces the normal airport outlook, warnings, timeline, detail navigation or source freshness. Its rows open the full trip; plane markers remain at this airport’s scheduled hours. Independent trips stay separate rather than implying a connection. The nav shell's Trips tab (`site/nav.js` calls `AWXTrips.render(container)`) lists every trip with "Add a trip" and the calendar line, or an empty state. Settings → Trips & flight calendar (`site/settings.js`, lines marked `// trips hook`) shows the calendar status from `AWXTrips.calStatus()` ("Connected · 3 upcoming flights", "Not connected", or that the last fetch failed), the calendar connection action, and the trips added on this device (`AWXTrips.list()`; each opens its trip sheet with Edit and Delete); the gear next to "Add a trip" opens it (without the nav shell, a Trips sheet in `site/trips.js` does the same). "Add a trip" (`AWXTrips.openAdd()`) takes from, to, an optional connection (with its landing and leaving times), the date and the local departure and arrival times; airports use the search component (`mountSearch`).

**Device setup.** Add a trip with airport-local scheduled times, or choose **Import a calendar file** in Trips or Settings. Export an `.ics` file from a flight/calendar app, choose it, review the airport/time-only preview, then save. Files up to 1 MB are parsed on the device; the source file, filename, event UID, flight numbers, names and booking details are never uploaded or saved. Only airports, scheduled times, zone hints and a local route/time id are saved in this browser. Matching routes and scheduled departures already on the device or repository calendar are skipped; reimporting does not overwrite saved times. Imports retain scheduled arrivals from the past 24 hours and departures in the next 7 days, ignore cancelled/all-day/unrecognized events, and group connecting legs. This is a one-time snapshot: it does not sync changes; edit/delete a saved trip or import again. Imported trips with more than one connection retain all legs; to update them, delete and import the new schedule (the manual editor supports one connection). Missing airport coverage remains qualified. Device storage failure reports that saving failed.

The Airports tab focuses on one next scheduled flight and its trip's most important concern, with one action to review the trip. Trips has the same focus followed by other trips. A passed scheduled time never confirms flight progress; recent schedules remain explicitly unconfirmed.

**Connect a flight calendar.** Trips and Settings → Trips & flight calendar both offer **Connect flight calendar**. Paste a public iCloud calendar subscription link (`webcal://p…-caldav.icloud.com/published/2/…`) or a Google Calendar ICS subscription link. For Flighty, enable Calendar Export in Settings → Calendar Sync, selecting a dedicated **iCloud** flight calendar. In Apple Calendar → Calendars → info beside that calendar, enable **Public Calendar → Share Link**, then paste that subscription link. Ordinary flight/friend share pages have no verified import format in this integration and are not supported; the form explains which link is needed. There is no GitHub or repository configuration in the app. Flighty’s [official Calendar Export help](https://flighty.com/help/calendar-export) confirms that it creates and maintains flight calendar events; its [calendar troubleshooting help](https://flighty.com/help/troubleshoot-calendar-sync) recommends one integration and one calendar per person to avoid duplicates.

The link and the sanitized latest schedule stay only in this browser (`awx-calendar-link`). A stateless `POST /calendar` on the existing live relay privately reads the calendar and returns only airport codes, scheduled times and route/time-derived SHA-256 IDs. It does not store the link or raw response, use a public cache, add trips to the build/history, or return names, UIDs, flight numbers or booking details. Shared calendars are readable by anyone with their link, so use a dedicated flight calendar. The relay requires the app's allowed origin and JSON, accepts only HTTPS iCloud published-calendar and Google Calendar ICS hosts/paths, validates each redirect, and limits request size (4 KB), calendar size (1 MB), and upstream time (12 s). The global airport lookup is packaged for international calendar recognition and time-zone interpretation. Unsupported links and failures produce generic errors without echoing personal URLs.

Connection refresh happens on opening the app, returning to it, and every ten minutes while visible. Refresh replaces the connection's schedule, including changed or removed flights; failures retain the last schedule with an explicit warning. Matching device flights are deduplicated. **Refresh now** forces a check; **Disconnect** removes the saved link and connected schedule, keeping manually saved/imported trips. There is no account or cross-device synchronization, and actual flight status is not supplied by this calendar connection. The existing owner calendar poller is retained for compatibility; connecting a device calendar supersedes that shared schedule on this device.

## History

The workflow keeps a `history` branch (orphan; created on the first run) with:

- `truth/YYYY/MM/DD.jsonl`: one line per poll with the observed outcomes: each airport's new METAR (skipped when its obsTime was already recorded), the FAA program state with cause classes, ATCSCC advisories that are active or new, and the FAA operations plan (national `opsplan` and each airport's `opsplan` items) on the first line with a new plan. Sources that failed are listed in `down`.
- `forecast/YYYY/MM/DD.jsonl`: one line per UTC hour (the first poll of the hour) with the predictions: TAF, LAMP, SPC, TCF, CWA, NWS alerts and the 24 hourly risk levels and reasons.
- `raw/latest/`: the first 200 KB of each source's latest raw response, overwritten every poll, plus `sources.json` (ok, error, http status, bytes, url, when captured). This is the place to check live formats.
- `README.md` describing all of it (written on first creation from `poller/record.mjs`).
- `movement/`: ADS-B traffic state, baseline and one log line per run (see "Movement"; `movement/README.md` describes the files).
- `changes/`: the change log's state (`changes/state.json`) and one line per event (`changes/YYYY/MM/DD.jsonl`; see "Change log and flight brief"; `changes/README.md` describes the files).

Lines are compact JSON (nulls/empties dropped, ISO times shortened). Locally: `node poller/poll.mjs --fixtures && node poller/record.mjs /tmp/history` (raw samples go to `.cache/raw`; `--raw <dir>` changes that). `poller/history.sh prepare|push` is the git side the workflow runs; it never creates an orphan branch unless the remote positively has no `history` branch, and on a rejected push rebases once and retries.

Note on size: raw/latest changes every poll, so its old versions accumulate in the branch's git history (a few hundred KB compressed per poll at most).

Aircraft data: **[adsb.fi](https://adsb.fi)** (primary) and **[ADSB.lol](https://adsb.lol)** (fallback; © ADSB.lol contributors, ODbL 1.0). Movement figures computed from ADSB.lol data (`movement.json`, the history log) are offered under the same ODbL 1.0 licence.

Sources (checked 2026-10-03; the sandbox couldn't reach the feeds or their websites, only their GitHub docs):

Replies are ADSBexchange-v2 style (`ac: [{hex, flight, lat, lon, alt_baro (ft or "ground"), alt_geom, gs, track, track_rate, baro_rate, geom_rate, category, seen, seen_pos}]`, field meanings from [readsb's README-json](https://github.com/wiedehopf/readsb/blob/dev/README-json.md)); the parser also accepts `aircraft: [...]`.

Each poll: one snapshot within 15 NM of each of the 32 airports, sequentially, at least 1.1 s apart per feed, 60 s budget (whatever didn't fit goes first next run; `state.next`); a failed adsb.fi request is retried on ADSB.lol, and after 3 adsb.fi failures in a row ADSB.lol goes first. That is 32 requests per run, ~9,200 a day at one run per 5 minutes. Dropped: non-ICAO (`~`) addresses, surface vehicles and obstacles (category C*), gliders/balloons/UAVs (B*), positions over 60 s old.

Classification against the field (height above field = `alt_baro` + METAR altimeter correction − field elevation; elevation from `airports-all.json` if it gets an elevation column, else the METAR `elev`, else the last known, else 0 ft, which the run log names):
- Ground: "ground", or under 100 ft above the field within 3.5 NM. Taxiing 5–40 kt, stationary under 5, landing/takeoff roll over 40. Ground aircraft farther out belong to another airport and are ignored.
- Departing: within 8 NM, under 4,000 ft, climbing over 500 fpm, track within 90° of the bearing away from the field.
- Arriving: within 12 NM, under 4,000 ft, descending over 300 fpm, track within 90° of the bearing to the field.
- Holding: within 40 NM at 4,000–20,000 ft and turning: the feed's `track_rate` at least 1.5°/s, else a 45°+ heading change since the last snapshot (≤ 15 min old) with under 40% of the expected progress. With 15 NM snapshots only holds close in are seen.
- Takeoff: a hex departing or on the ground (moving, and not just landed) last time that is departing, airborne or gone now. Landing: arriving last time and on the ground or gone now, or first seen on the landing roll. A parked aircraft that disappears (transponder off) is not a takeoff; a go-around isn't a departure.

Rates: each departure/arrival hex is counted once per hour. `depHr`/`arrHr` = unique hexes over the last 60 minutes ÷ coverage (snapshots in that hour ÷ 12, capped at 1). Finished clock hours with coverage ≥ 0.5 become baseline samples per airport × hour of week (local time, 0 = Sunday 00), newest 4 kept, with departures per hub airline. Baseline, first available: our own log (median, at least 3 samples) → BTS scheduled departures for that hour of week, if the Phase 3 output in `site/data/model/` has them (`schedule.json`, or a `sched`/`schedule` object `{IATA: {dep: [168 or 24], arr}}` in `model.json`/`fallback.json`; none today) → none ("Learning normal traffic (N of 21 days)"). `index` = depHr ÷ baseline depHr (null under 3 departures/hr normal).

`site/data/movement.json` (git-ignored, written every run): `{v, generated, run: {ok, airports, of, ms, outOfTime, src, errors, failed}, sources, learning: {since, days, of}, airports: {IATA: {depHr, arrHr, taxiOut, holding, asOf, coverage, n, raw: {dep, arr}, baseline: {depHr, arrHr, n, source: "own"|"bts"|null}, index, sentence, stale?, elev?}}, airlineAlerts: [{airline, name, hubs, sentence}]}`. Sentences: "Departures 38/hr vs 61 normal (↓38%)" when the index is under 0.7 or over 1.3, "Moving normally", "Limited data this hour (5 of 12 checks)" under 0.6 coverage, "Learning normal traffic (N of 21 days)".

Airline alerts: a carrier (`AIRLINES` in `poller/movement.mjs`: majors with hubs, plus regional and cargo codes) whose departures were under 30% of its own baseline at 3+ of its hubs in each of the last 2 finished hours (coverage ≥ 0.6, its baseline ≥ 3/hr there).

Page (`site/movement.js`, `window.AWXMovement`): the card line in plain words by departures index — 0 (none at all, e.g. a closed airport) "No departures", under 0.3 "Departures almost stopped", 0.3–0.6 "Departures far below normal", 0.6–0.8 "Departures below normal", 1.3–1.6 "Busier than normal", 1.6+ "Much busier than normal", nothing in between (exact "38 vs 61 per hour" only in Aviation mode and the card's detail rows; also requires coverage ≥ 0.6, a baseline and data under 20 minutes old); the menu-opened "Traffic right now" page (departures and arrivals per hour as bars with a tick at normal, taxiing out, holding nearby, as-of time, source line "From community ADS-B receivers (adsb.fi) · baseline: our log, 12 days"); airline alerts as a banner above the list (or in `#natstrip` when that exists). `SOURCES` is exported for Settings → Data & checks; `check.html` shows a "Movement feed" row (warns, never fails).

History (`movement/` on the `history` branch): the workflow copies `state.json`/`baseline.json` to `.cache/movement/` before the poll and `node poller/movement.mjs record history/` writes them back with one log line per run (`movement/YYYY/MM/DD.jsonl`: `{t, src, skip?, airports: {IATA: {dep, arr, taxi, ground, holding, coverage, byAirline}}}`, dep/arr = first counted that run). Both steps are `continue-on-error`. Fixtures: `poller/fixtures/movement/` (ORD snapshots 5 minutes apart, moved to every airport, plus `seed.json` with a seeded history: ORD below normal, LAS above, ANC/HNL learning, a United alert).

## Change log and flight brief

**Change log** (`poller/changes.mjs`, `poller/changes.test.mjs`; steps marked `brief hook` in `poll.yml`). Each run compares the new status.json (and movement.json) with the previous run's state (`changes/state.json` on the history branch, copied to `.cache/changes/` before the poll the way movement's state is) and emits per-airport events `{t, iata, kind, from, to, sentence, cause?, prog?}`: risk level up/down ("Risk up to High (storms)"), FAA program started / ended / extended (ground stop, delay program, FAA delays; NAS status or an active ATCSCC GS/GDP: "Ground stop started (storms)", "Ground stop lifted", "Ground stop extended until 5:30 PM"), full airport closure start/end, a new NWS warning ("Severe Thunderstorm Warning issued"), the near-term delay word changing ("Delays possible → Delays likely"; words from `site/delay.js` `likelihood()` for the current and next two hours, logged only when one side is "Delays likely" or stronger and never to/from "Delays happening now"), the ops plan adding or dropping a possible ground stop, and departures moving below/above/back to normal (movement.json, only fresh entries with coverage ≥ 0.6 and a baseline). **Observed backfill** (`observedEvents`, `obs: true`): the timeline's past hours come from each hour's reports (status `observed`, the worst METAR/SPECI in the hour) while level events follow the poller's current level at each poll, so every completed observed hour whose level differs from the hour before also gets a level event, timed at the hour's start, unless a logged level change in the same direction already falls between the start of the earlier hour and the end of the later one (each logged change covers one). Short spells (a gusty hour) and gaps between polls (a missed or late build) still reach the log, and the log ties out to the timeline; each hour is looked at once (state `obs`). Level, word and movement changes are debounced: one that reverts within 10 minutes is ignored, and a confirmed one is timed when first seen. A source that failed (FAA, ATCSCC, NWS, or no national ops plan) keeps its previous state, so an outage never reads as "lifted". Sentences carry no time of their own (the page prefixes it). Output: `site/data/changes.json` = `{v, generated, since, events}` (last 36 h, newest first; deployed with the page, git-ignored; `{error, events: []}` when the step failed) and, via `node poller/changes.mjs record history/`, `changes/YYYY/MM/DD.jsonl` (UTC day of the event; a line already there isn't added again). Locally: `node poller/poll.mjs --fixtures && node poller/changes.mjs --fixtures` (a simulated quiet previous state, or `poller/fixtures/changes-prev.json`). Scenario builds write `site/data/scenarios/<name>/changes.json` the same way; a scenario's `changes-prev.json` (template tokens allowed) overrides airports' previous state (`{at, airports: {IATA: {lvl: {v}, faa: {type: {end, cause}}, adv, closed, warn, plan, word, mv}}, recent: [events]}`) — `recovering` uses it for the lifted EWR ground stop.

**Flight brief.** `site/trips.js` shows one compact **Today's flight brief** entry on the Airports tab and in the menu only on a leg's scheduled departure date in the origin airport's zone. It opens the trip sheet with the overall schedule-based outlook, followed by **Along your itinerary** sections for departure, connection and arrival airports with concerns or unavailable forecast coverage. Quiet airports add no concern section; starred or unrelated airports never enter the brief. The scheduled itinerary and airport detail links remain available. Calendar update failures and unconfirmed actual flight status remain explicit. `site/brief.js` retains the airport menu’s **Today’s changes** page: events since airport-local midnight in the display zone, newest first, six at most, with hidden categories left out. Check page: Change log data quality, `change`, `noAirportBrief`, `today`, and day-of flight brief UI checks.

Today’s Brief is one tap target with up to three summary lines. It opens one scrollable sheet with up to three relevant airport cards, rendered by the same card component as the main list. Each card can open its airport details; the shared sheet controller handles Back, drag dismissal, scroll lock and transitions.

## Terminal maps and lounges

Two cards near the bottom of every major airport's sheet (`site/terminals.js`; hooks marked `terminals hook` in `app.js`, `index.html`, `check.js` and `poller/run-tests.mjs`).

**Terminal map.** A small north-up SVG of the airport's terminal outlines, gates as dots, runways as thin lines, OpenStreetMap lounges as amber diamonds and the concourse letters. Tap it for a full-screen sheet (`site/sheet.js` `makeSheet`): pan, pinch, wheel and double-tap zoom (the SVG viewBox, no library), gate labels once neighbouring gates are ~26 px apart, gate search ("B12", "gate b 12", or a concourse letter) that highlights and centres the gate and names its concourse and terminal, and a link to the airport's own map page (or its website when no map page is recorded). The Airport details menu opens the full-screen viewer directly. Only airports listed in `site/data/terminals/index.json` show a Terminal map row; the selected airport file loads on demand.

Data: `tools/build-terminals.mjs` (pure helpers in `tools/terminals-lib.mjs`) runs **one** Overpass API query per airport (`https://overpass-api.de/api/interpreter`): the aerodrome way/relation tagged with the airport's IATA or ICAO code becomes the search area, and inside it `aeroway=terminal`, `building=terminal`, `aeroway=gate`, `aeroway=runway`, `amenity=lounge` and `aeroway=lounge`, with inline geometry. Outlines are projected to local metres around the aerodrome centre (so the drawing is north-up), simplified with Douglas-Peucker at 5 m, multipolygon pieces joined, pieces under 300 m² dropped, and unnamed `building=terminal` pieces (concourse fingers) attached to the nearest named terminal within 150 m. Gates are grouped by the letter their ref starts with ("Concourse C") and the terminal they sit in (or nearest within 400 m); a duplicated ref is kept once. Output `site/data/terminals/<IATA>.json` = `{v, iata, origin [lat, lon], osmBase, aerodrome, bbox, terminals [{name, of?, rings}], groups [{name, terminal, gates}], gates [{ref, x, y, g}], runways [{ref, line}], lounges [{name, op?, x, y}], attribution, license, licenseUrl, ok}` (x east, y north, metres), and `index.json` = `{v, checked, airports: {IATA: {ok, terminals, gates, runways, lounges, osmBase, updated, fixture?, lastError?}}, ok, failed}`. `.github/workflows/terminals.yml` ("Terminal maps") runs it monthly (3rd, 07:41 UTC) and on dispatch (optional `only` list), at most 32 queries a month, one at a time, 5.5 s apart, 60 s timeout, no retries, with an identifying User-Agent, far inside the Overpass usage policy. It's tolerant: a failed or empty airport keeps its previous file (`lastError` in the index) and the run exits 0 unless every airport failed. An airport file is rewritten only when its content changed (the OSM timestamp alone doesn't count); `index.json` carries the run's check date, so a successful run commits it monthly. Locally: `node tools/build-terminals.mjs --fixtures` builds MSP from `tools/fixtures/terminals/MSP.overpass.json` (hand-made, realistic layout: the committed MSP file is that sample, flagged `fixture: true` and labelled "Sample layout" on the card until the first workflow run replaces it).

Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License ([openstreetmap.org/copyright](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright)); the card and the full-screen map both show this attribution with the link.

**Lounges.** `site/data/lounges.json` is **hand-curated and may be out of date**: lounges open, close, move and change their entry rules. Per airport `{map: {url, kind: "map"|"home", verified}, lounges: [{name, operator, terminal, area?, side: "airside"|"landside", access, verified, source, confidence: "high"|"low"}]}`; `access` is one plain line (≤ 60 characters, no "%"), `source` the operator's lounge page, `verified` the date someone checked that page (YYYY-MM-DD, or null), and "high" confidence requires a verified date. ## Backtest (baseline accuracy)

`.github/workflows/backtest.yml` (manual: Actions -> "Backtest baseline" -> Run workflow; inputs `months`, default 2, and `airports`, default `all`) replays history through the TAF rules in `poller/risk.mjs`:

- Period: the latest `months` months with a published BTS On-Time zip (current month and the 5 before it are probed); if none is found, the last complete months, weather only.
- Forecasts: historical TAFs from IEM (`cgi-bin/request/taf.py`), parsed by `poller/taf-parse.mjs` into AWC JSON shape and scored per hour with `tafHour`, at lead buckets 0-3, 3-6, 6-12 and 12-24 h (latest TAF issued at or before H - lead). TAF rules only: no FAA/NWS/SPC/SIGMET history.
- Truth: IEM METAR/SPECI (`asos.py`) scored with the same `assessConditions` thresholds (max over the hour), and BTS departures per local scheduled hour (disrupted = >= 20% of departures 15+ min late with weather/NAS delay, or >= 5% weather cancellations; >= 5 departures).
- Output: `reports/baseline-YYYY-MM-DD.md` + `.json` and `reports/samples/` (first 50 KB of the raw IEM TAF/METAR CSVs and the BTS header) committed to the `history` branch and uploaded as an artifact. POD/FAR/CSI/bias per level threshold and phenomenon, confusion matrices, disruption reliability table, persistence and climatology baselines.

Phase 3 fixed the IEM row-per-group TAF rebuild: validity is now 30 h from the issue hour (or the latest group end if later). Before, it ended at the last TEMPO/PROB group's end, so later hours of such TAFs went unscored (the Oct 3 2026 baseline was made with that bug).

Locally: `node tools/backtest.mjs --fixtures` (reads `tools/fixtures/`, writes to the system temp dir unless `--out`). `node poller/run-tests.mjs` runs all unit tests, including `tools/`.

## Delay model

The risk level says *what* weather is coming; the delay model answers "is this likely to cause a real delay?" with a calibrated chance per airport and hour, the usual chance for that hour, the expected delay length and a plain historical comparison ("In 214 similar evening hours at ORD since Aug 2024, 131 (61%) had delays of 15+ min; median 38 min."). The risk levels are unchanged; `delay` sits next to them in every hour.

**Real delay hour (the target).** In that local hour at the airport, at least 25% of scheduled departures *or* of scheduled arrivals were 15+ min late with BTS `WeatherDelay` or `NASDelay` minutes, or at least 5% of them were cancelled for weather/NAS (`CancellationCode` B or C). A side (departures by Origin + CRSDepTime, arrivals by Dest + CRSArrTime, next day when the arrival clock is earlier than the departure clock) counts only with >= 5 flights; hours where neither side has 5 are skipped. Also kept per hour: flights, shares, median and 90th-percentile minutes of late flights, mean delay. Caveat: BTS gives causes only for flights *arriving* 15+ min late, and NAS includes volume/equipment as well as weather.

**Data** (`tools/train-data.mjs`). The last 24 months with BTS published (two winters). Per airport and lead bucket (0-3, 3-6, 6-12, 12-24 h; the latest TAF issued at or before H - lead, at most 30 h old) the IEM TAF (`taf.py`, row-per-group CSV, parsed by `poller/taf-parse.mjs`) is replayed with `tafHourParts`/`tafHour` from `poller/risk.mjs`. IEM requests are <= 1/s with retries/backoff (5–180 s, Retry-After honoured), chunked by 3 months (falling back to single months) and cached in the run; samples of every raw format go to the artifact and `reports/samples-train/`.

**Features** (`poller/delay.mjs` `tafSummary`/`condSummary`/`encode`, shared by training and live scoring): rule level (TAF only) and level x lead; ceiling and visibility categories; thunder (prevailing TS, prevailing VCTS, TEMPO, PROB30/40), heavy thunder, thunder x lead and x time of day; gust bins (15/25/35 kt, PROB-only gusts), wind 20+ kt; crosswind on the best runway (runway headings from `site/data/airports-all.json`, stored in the model) in 10/15/25 kt bins; precip type RA/SN/heavy SN/FZ/PL (prevailing+TEMPO, and PROB-only); TEMPO IFR; hour, weekday, month; airport; lead; the persistence observation (latest METAR at most 3 h before the time the forecast is made: level, thunder, IFR, wintry precip, gusts, crosswind, each x lead); hub cascade (`HUBS`: each airport's main connecting hub, e.g. MSP -> ORD, BOS -> EWR: thunder, PROB thunder, IFR, snow/ice, gusts, level High+ at the hub); and the airport x month x hour climatology as a continuous input. Optional (`--lamp`, off by default): IEM LAMP archive LP1/CP1 and ceiling/visibility categories (`mos.py?model=LAV`, checked against real KORD midnight and 06Z CSVs in `tools/fixtures/train/`). Date-only midnight runtimes parse as UTC midnight; invalid calendar dates are rejected. The [IEM archive](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/mos/) has only 00/06/12/18Z LAV cycles since July 12, 2020. [IEM’s parser](https://github.com/akrherz/pyIEM/blob/fc358a889315cea0c8fd89cdda6a38840bc69a05/src/pyiem/nws/products/mos.py#L47) drops the bulletin’s :30 issuance minutes: historical lookup restores that offset, selects a cycle issued by the prediction time and rejects cycles older than six hours. Dataset metadata records this timing policy. Actual receipt timestamps are absent, so issuance is a lower bound on availability; replay must not claim exact live delivery or hourly live LAMP coverage.

**Model** (`tools/train.mjs` -> `site/data/model/model.json`). L2-regularised logistic regression on the one-hot features with lead as a feature, fitted by IRLS (Newton) in plain JS; the global intercept and the climatology term are unpenalised and per-airport intercepts shrink toward the global one. Lambda (1/10/100) is chosen by out-of-fold log loss over three contiguous time blocks of the training months, and predictions from a fit on the earlier training months fit isotonic calibration (PAV, <= 60 knots) on the final two pre-test training months. The final weights are fitted on all training months; the probability mapping stays fixed before test evaluation. Expected minutes: median late-flight minutes among real-delay hours by airport x predicted-probability decile (global decile when an airport has < 15 hours).

**Validation.** Strict time split: test = the newest 4 months; when none of them is December–February the test block reaches back to the newest winter month (at most 8 months); training = everything before. Reported on the test months: Brier score and skill vs (a) climatology by airport x hour x month and (b) the current rule levels mapped to their historical rates (lead x level, training months); AUC; a 10-bin reliability table (also for the rule mapping); catching real delay hours (model >= 50%/30% vs rule High+/Moderate+); hours where the model said much higher/lower than usual vs what happened; per lead, season and airport; error of the expected minutes. Displayed likelihood uses that fixed model calibration directly; `displaySupport` freezes support/caps on calibration months, and `test.displayWords` reports sample counts, mean prediction and observed outcomes for each resulting word on the test rows. No test outcomes fit a new display mapping. Output: `site/data/model/report.json` (read by `site/accuracy.html`) and `reports/model-YYYY-MM-DD.md/.json` on the history branch.

**Safety gate.** The workflow writes `site/data/model/model.json` only if test Brier skill vs climatology > 0 **and** the model's Brier beats the rule-level mapping (and there are >= 200 test hours). Otherwise the previous model stays and the report says "not deployed" (with the reasons) and which model is live. `fallback.json`, `report.json` and `analogs/` are always updated.

**Fallback.** Without a usable `model.json` (none yet, or built for another feature `SPEC`), the scorer uses `fallback.json`: the TAF rule level -> its historical real-delay rate per lead bucket (computed in every training run from all months), the climatology for "usually X%", and median minutes per level. Until the first training run the committed `fallback.json` is a seed from the June–July 2026 baseline backtest (departures only, >= 20% definition; marked `source: "seed"`; "usual" is the airport's rate then), so the page always has a historically grounded number.

**Historical comparison (analogs).** Every past hour (lead 0-3 h forecast) gets a signature: dominant hazard (storms > snow/ice > low cloud/fog > wind > clear) x severity x time of day (morning 5–11, afternoon 12–16, evening 17–21, overnight) x season. `site/data/model/analogs/<IATA>.json` stores per bucket `[hours, real-delay hours, median minutes, weather/NAS cancellation share]` (buckets with >= 15 hours). The sentence uses the finest bucket with >= 15 hours; coarser buckets drop the time of day (then season) and say "hours with similar conditions".

**FAA overrides.** A full airport closure sets p = 1 (`override: "closure"`, "Delays happening now", never "Delays very likely") in every hour of its window (start through reopening, as the risk rules; hour 0 only with no known end), and wins over any program in those hours. An FAA ground stop or ground delay program in effect (NAS status, active ATCSCC advisory, or an active ops-plan program) sets p = 1 ("delays happening now") through its stated end, held 3 h (5 h when increasing) without one like the risk rules; minutes = the FAA's stated average delay when given (`minutesFrom: "faa"`). A general FAA delay with a range of 15+ min sets p = 1 in hour 0, and through its end when the FAA gives one (minutes = mid-range). An ops-plan "GROUND STOP/DELAY PROGRAM POSSIBLE" raises p to at least the historical rate at which such possible programs became real ones at that airport within their window, computed from the `history` branch's `truth/` log in each training run (`fallback.programs`, needs >= 5 cases), else 0.5 (`rateFrom: history|default`); it never lowers p.

**Live scoring.** `poller/delay.mjs` `scoreHours` is pure ESM: `poll.mjs` loads `site/data/model/` from disk (`loadDelayModel`, env `DELAY_MODEL_DIR` overrides) and `core.mjs` `assemble` adds `hours[].delay` (marked `// phase3 hook`). The live relay fetches `data/model/model.json`, `fallback.json` and the requested airports' analogs from the Pages site (kept 6 h; a 404 is cached too), scores with the same function (hubs it wasn't asked for use the build's TAF), and carries over the build's numbers if the model files can't be fetched.

**Page.** `site/delay.js` (module, `window.AWXDelay`) supplies the shared delay words. Airport details omit routine delay rates; a forecast outlook appears for weather/program risk or a calibrated rate of at least 45% that is higher than usual. It shows the timing, typical duration, and source basis. Aviation mode also shows calibration and analog text without expanders. Normal aircraft movement is omitted in Traveler sheets; unusual movement gets a brief status and update time. `site/accuracy.html` and `site/accuracy.js` explain reliability, comparisons, sample sizes and model performance.

**Retraining.** Actions -> "Train delay model" (`.github/workflows/train.yml`; also monthly on the 22nd). Inputs: `months` (24), `airports` (all), `test_months` (4), `lamp` (false), `publish` (true). Runtime ~2–3.5 h (BTS zips ~30–45 min, IEM ~1.5–2.5 h at 1 request/s, fitting a few minutes); timeout 300 min. Locally: `node tools/train.mjs --fixtures` runs the whole pipeline on a synthetic world (`tools/train-fixtures.mjs`, real file formats; FIXTURE in every output) into the temp dir, or `--out <dir>`; `node tools/train-data.mjs bts|build …` and `node tools/train.mjs --data <dir>` are the real steps.

**Bounded LAMP research.** Start with a non-publishing candidate (`publish: false`) on ORD, MSP and SFO, 24 months, `lamp: true`, other optional families off, `ablation: true`, `test_months: 4`. This is a research subset and cannot replace the 32-airport deployment. The workflow artifacts already include `data/dataset.jsonl.gz` and `data/meta.json`; reuse matching archived datasets for repeated fits rather than downloading all sources again. Compare the base and base + LAMP variants on the identical untouched months and retain the strict deployed-model/climatology/rule gate. A parser fix or synthetic ablation is not evidence of real accuracy improvement; a successful research pilot still needs a full-coverage candidate and source-timing audit before deployment. No new model is shipped by the parser/display fix.

## MRMS research pilot

`tools/mrms-pilot.mjs` is a manual, artifact-only NOAA MRMS collector. It shares one `MergedReflectivityQCComposite_00.50` CONUS grid across all selected airports (default ORD/MSP/SFO; maximum 32) and summarizes 25 km airport and 50 km surrounding circles. These are approximate geographic buffers, not flight approach corridors. Upwind direction is omitted without contemporaneous wind. No browser fetch, live risk rule, model feature, `SPEC` change or prediction is added.

The genuine July 1 and October 4, 2026 objects use regular latitude/longitude GRIB2 template 3.0 and PNG packing 5.41 (7000 × 3500, grayscale 16-bit). `tools/mrms-lib.mjs` validates sections, PNG CRCs/filters, dimensions and packing; supports an explicit bitmap; rejects unsupported templates. Runtime dependencies are Node built-ins only. One grid request is capped at 4 MB compressed; two date-boundary listings at 100 KB each, each at most 32 objects; decompression/grid bounds are 80 MB/25 million cells. No retries or pagination loops. The inherited HTTPS proxy and CA trust are preserved when present.

```
node tools/mrms-pilot.mjs --at 2026-07-01T00:02:00Z --airports ORD,MSP,SFO,DEN --quality --out /tmp/mrms-frame.json
node tools/mrms-pilot.mjs --out /tmp/mrms-next.json --previous /tmp/mrms-frame.json
node --test tools/mrms.test.mjs
```

Output records GRIB source time, S3 `LastModified` publication proxy, actual receipt time, object SHA-256/ETag, expiry and per-buffer coverage. Selection requires publication and source time at or before the requested cutoff; frames expire after 15 minutes. Reports include maximum observed reflectivity, affected fractions at 35/45 dBZ where at least 80% of the buffer has valid numeric data, and a 5–30-minute change when both frames are comparable. Missing fractions remain null; partial observations include explicit lower/upper bounds.

**Missing is unknown.** The [NOAA MRMS product table](https://github.com/NOAA-National-Severe-Storms-Laboratory/mrms-support/blob/main/GRIB2_TABLES/UserTable_MRMS_v12.2.csv) labels reflectivity −99 as Missing and −999 as No Coverage. Neither becomes zero or an all-clear claim. `--quality` optionally fetches one aligned `RadarQualityIndex_00.00` grid (8-bit PNG, category 8/parameter 0), published by the same cutoff and within two minutes of the reflectivity frame. Its −1/−3 sentinels remain unavailable. Quality is a diagnostic: the verified table does not establish that a valid quality index makes missing reflectivity a valid no-echo observation. That semantic/coverage gap must be resolved before an all-condition prediction study.

**Evaluation.** `tools/mrms-evaluate.mjs --data <dataset-dir> --frames <compact-json-dir> --out <report.json>` causally joins artifact features to BTS airport-hour weather/NAS outcomes and the deployed base-only comparator. The packed dataset supports exact prediction cutoffs of 0 and 3 hours; it does not reconstruct 1/2-hour forecasts. Pilot thresholds and regularization are prespecified. On identical matched held-out rows it compares base plus MRMS against a base refit without MRMS, the deployed model, training-only climatology and rule rates; reports Brier, AUC, calibration, false alarms, airport/lead coverage and sample support. Fit/calibration/test blocks each need at least 200 matched rows, 20 examples of each outcome, eight distinct dates, 20 observed-storm and 20 observed-nonstorm rows, and at least 80% numeric coverage within the declared airport/time cohort. These support requirements are frozen in code. A research pass additionally requires lower Brier than every comparator, ECE no worse than the deployed model and base refit control, and a strict ECE improvement against at least one of those two. Improvement against an already perfectly calibrated control is not mathematically required, but worsening either reference blocks the gate. `--sample-hours 0,12` can predeclare systematic verifying-hour sampling for a bounded archive replay (default all UTC hours); declare the cohort before collection/evaluation, never select hours from known storms/outcomes. Input is capped at 5000 compact frame files. Insufficient coverage returns `insufficient-data` and a failing gate; it never invents scores. A research pass is not permission or machinery to deploy a model. Existing historic holdouts were used in prior model selection; repeated leads share outcomes; S3 publication does not reconstruct a historical receiver's latency. Full coverage and a future untouched evaluation are still needed.

Actions → **MRMS research pilot** is dispatch-only: two shared frames 15 minutes apart, optionally two quality grids, at most 8 MB compressed without quality/16 MB with it, plus bounded listings. It uploads compact research artifacts for 30 days; it has read-only repository permissions, no schedule, no new secret and no live output. Do not select only storm cases for retrospective collection: representative quiet/disrupted periods across fit, calibration and untouched test months are necessary to measure incremental value. The initial genuine current/archive probes establish decoding and publication metadata, but their sparse/unknown coverage cannot establish an accuracy gain. The committed real DEN crop is 207 bytes, losslessly derived from the genuine archive PNG and independently checked; full grids stay outside the repository.

## Plain English (`poller/plain.mjs`)

Pure ESM with no imports, used by the poller and (as the byte-identical copy `site/plain.js`) by the page. Approach: one source file, committed copy; `poller/plain.test.mjs` fails if the copy drifts (`cp poller/plain.mjs site/plain.js`).

- `plainMetar(metar)`, `plainTafHour(fcstGroup)`: traveler sentences in mph, miles and plain cloud words ("Rain and low clouds", "Thunderstorms with heavy rain", "Gusts to 40 mph", "Fog — visibility under 1 mile"; TEMPO = "At times …", PROB30 = "30% chance of …"). Accepts AWC JSON or the status.json `metar` shape.
- `travelerImpact(level, conditions)`: what it means for flights, worded as possible/likely, never certain ("Arrivals are often slowed in these conditions; delays of 30+ min possible", "Storms can pause departures and arrivals (ground stops) — expect delays", "Strong gusty winds may cause delays or diversions" (gusts ≥ 35 kt; no runway or crosswind claim), "De-icing and slower operations — delays likely" (freezing precipitation, ice pellets or falling snow; blowing/drifting snow, BLSN/DRSN, alone doesn't count), "Flights operating normally"). FAA programs in `conditions.faa` take precedence; GA-only (`scope: limited`) closures don't count.
- `plainSigmet(s, {tz})` / `plainCwa(c, {tz})`: "Area of severe thunderstorms moving east at 25 mph, tops to 45,000 ft, until 9 PM" (movement is the direction storms move toward). The CWA text field name is unverified (`cwaText`, `rawText`, `text` or `raw` are tried).
- `plainAlert(alert, {tz, now})`: "Winter Storm Warning until Sun 6 AM", "Wind Advisory, 2–8 PM".
- `aviationLines(metar | fcst)`: pilot lines for aviation mode (flight category, ceiling ft AGL, visibility sm, wind dir/speed/gust kt, decoded weather codes, cloud layers, temp/dew point).

Not yet wired into status.json's own fields (that happens when the app gets aviation mode); `poller/global.mjs` already uses it for searched airports.

## Search and the full airport list

The At risk list, airport search and Map monitor **588 U.S. and territory airline-service airports**. `poller/airports.mjs` merges the original 32-airport baseline with scheduled-service U.S./territory fields having an ICAO code, time zone and AWC METAR capability. METAR/TAF queries are batched into at most 100 station ids; observation history uses 10 ids to avoid response record caps. Airports without a TAF retain qualified/unknown forecasts; unsupported airports remain searchable with weather-only or no-report wording. The monitored set refreshes with the weekly catalog; no manually maintained regional shortlist limits it. International scheduled-service airports remain searchable with explicitly weather-only coverage.

The original 32-airport list remains the stable fixture, model-training, terminal-map and traffic-monitoring baseline. Expansion does not invent lounge data, terminal maps, ADS-B monitoring, hub-route relationships, or per-airport calibration for new airports. - `tools/build-airports.mjs` downloads OurAirports `airports.csv` + `runways.csv` and AWC's `stations.cache.json.gz`, and writes `site/data/airports-all.json`: every airport worldwide with scheduled airline service, plus every US airport (incl. PR, GU, VI, AS, MP, UM) of type small/medium/large with an ICAO or GPS code; no heliports, seaplane bases, balloonports or closed airports. Ranking: exact code; alias table (NYC → JFK/LGA/EWR, Chicago → ORD/MDW, DC → DCA/IAD/BWI, Bay Area → SFO/OAK/SJC, London, …; 4+ letters match alias prefixes); scheduled service first; larger types first; then code prefix, city word prefix, name word prefix.
- `site/searched.js` wires it into the page: a major opens its normal sheet; any other airport gets a card from the global shards with "Weather only — airport disruption data unavailable" (US) or "Weather only — FAA/NWS data covers U.S. airports" (international); an airport with no METAR/TAF says "No weather reports from this airport — check the nearest major airport" and links the nearest airport with reports. Starred non-major airports share the `awx-favs` list and show on My airports.

### Shared travel outlook and risk map

`site/outlook.js` evaluates the same airport-hour outlook for the detail sheet and Map. Forecast wording describes airport disruption risk, not the probability that an individual flight will be delayed. Directional FAA impact, reported delay trends, scheduled restriction end times, and the FAA’s published extension outlook appear as brief static rows when available. Forecast improvement is separate from a restriction’s scheduled end, and is left out when a program in force has a cause other than weather (volume, staffing, equipment, an airline's IT outage…). "Lower disruption risk forecast after X" appears only when the level falls to Low/Clear (≤ 1) and the next hour confirms it; a drop that stays disruptive reads "Eases to Moderate after X" (`evaluate().eases`). Neither falls inside the delay window shown above it (the outlook's own window or the sheet's delay line, `notBefore`): the first confirmed hour after that window's end is used. Hours with no forecast never confirm. The condition headline puts storms and winter weather before visibility; "Dense fog" needs fog or mist (in the reasons or the hour's weather codes) and visibility ≤ 1/2 sm (fractions incl. 1/8, 3/8 and "1 1/2" are read as numbers); other visibility ≤ 1/2 sm is "Low visibility". Hours no forecast covers (level null) are unknown in evaluate, summary, windows, the Map and Trips, never Clear. Trip context flags overlap with the card's level window. No flight outcome or cancellation feed is implied.

**Display level (one level everywhere).** Each hour has one display level (`score()` / `levelAt()` in `site/outlook.js`): the hour's weather/FAA level raised by its delay words — "Delays happening now" to the FAA program's level (ground stop or closure Severe, delay program High, delays Moderate), "Delays very likely" to at least High, "Delays likely" to at least Moderate, "Delays possible" to at least Minor; usual delays, a small chance and "unlikely" raise nothing — and by FAA restrictions in force. Timeline segments (cards, the sheet, the lens and its label), hour cards, the Map's dots, the brief's windows and trip mini-timelines all use it. `summary()` gives the airport's one level (the highest remaining hour), its window (the first run at that level), the current run and what follows; the sheet's Looking ahead section, lists' sorting and At risk, national panel, brief and trips read it. The home card's badge, reason, accessibility label and data-level use only the current outlook, matching the detail sheet's Now headline. Future risk summaries and timing appear in a Looking ahead section below the detail timeline and above Today so far, using the same log-card rows and colors; hourly timelines retain their forecast colors. An hour raised by the delay chance (or with nothing else to say) says why: "Busy evening — delays likely", "Delays at ORD may spread here", "FAA ground stop in effect". A caption identifies future hours as forecasts. An FAA program with no stated end reads "<Level> — FAA gives no end time" (the poller's 3–5 hour hold isn't an end); future hours retain the existing hourly forecast colors, with the caption "Future hours are forecast estimates; FAA end time is unknown." Held-hour previews explicitly identify the forecast and retain the unknown-end qualifier when forecast risk falls below the current program level. Forecast improvement is labeled "Forecast estimate" and describes lower forecast disruption risk, not a confirmed program end. The check page asserts, for every scenario and live data: card pill = current sheet headline, the headline's hours are coloured at its level, every coloured hour has a reason, no "null"/"undefined"/"NaN" and no Traveler "%".

**Stale data.** Over 20 minutes since the data shown was fetched (the live relay's time, else the build's): every card shows a muted "May be outdated" by its pill and the sheet's first line reads "Last updated N min ago — may be outdated". When the live relay fails the header reads "Live updates unavailable — showing the latest build".

### Map tab (`site/map.js`)

- **What it shows.** A full-height map (mainland U.S. at first; Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico and any starred airport abroad by panning or zooming) with one dot per airport: every airport in status.json (the 32 majors and trip airports) plus starred and trip airports outside it, coloured from their global shard entry (`data/wx/`, `AWXExtra._shardFor`; weather only). The colour is the shared airport-hour outlook (`AWXApp.outlook`, `site/outlook.js`) at the chosen hour, so the map, the list and the sheet agree; missing, stale or incomplete coverage is a hollow grey dot ("No data"), never green. Severe dots pulse gently (a still halo under reduced motion). IATA labels (ICAO with the Airport codes setting) are placed worst airport first, right/left/above/below the dot, skipping any that would overlap a dot, another label or the floating controls. A High or Severe airport off screen gets a pointer at the edge; tapping it pans there.
- **Controls.** Segmented All / My airports (starred + trip airports) / At risk (Moderate or worse, an FAA program, or no data), Overlays (FAA programs: an amber ring around airports with a ground stop, delay program or closure at that hour), zoom +/−, "U.S." (back to the mainland view) and "List". Choices are kept in localStorage `awx-map`. The time slider runs from 12 hours back (when `observed` history is there; past hours use the timeline's observed slots, `AWXApp.daySlots`) to the last forecast hour (+23 h), labelled "Now", "Tonight 9 PM", "Tomorrow 6 AM", "Today 3 PM" following Settings → Times: the device's zone ("your time") with My time zone, else the first starred airport's zone (named "MSP time"). Legend: Clear, Minor, Moderate, High, Severe (and No data when shown). Trip routes are dashed lines between airport codes only.
- **Height.** The map takes about two-thirds of the screen (at least 340 px), so the airport list shows below it and the page scrolls easily.
- **Radar overlay.** Overlays → Radar (off by default, remembered): NOAA's quality-controlled MRMS reflectivity (SeamlessHSR) from the Iowa Environmental Mesonet's tile cache (`mrms::lcref-<time>`, latest time from `lcref.json`, re-checked every 2 minutes), decoded to dBZ with IEM's gr2ae table and redrawn in one neutral ramp (dark slate in light mode, pale blue-white in dark; more opaque for heavier echoes, nothing under 10 dBZ) so it never competes with the risk colours. Drawn into the base canvas under state lines and labels, for the current hour only (the legend says "Radar shows now only" at other hours, "Radar unavailable" if IEM can't be reached). U.S. mainland only. Code: `site/map/mrms.js`.
- **Gestures.** Drag to pan, pinch or wheel to zoom, double-tap to zoom in, tap a dot to open its sheet (`AWXApp.openSheet`; non-majors open their card on the Airports tab). Hit radius 22 px with a mouse (44 px target), 30 px under a finger. The stage takes arrow keys and +/−.
- **Drawing.** Two canvases: the base map and an overlay (dots, rings, lines, labels) redrawn every frame it changes. During a gesture the base canvas follows by CSS transform and is redrawn at most every 180 ms, then at the end. The base map is `site/map/vmap.js`, a copy of the weather site's `wx-vmap.js` (OpenFreeMap vector tiles, OpenMapTiles schema, decoded in a worker; local changes: `drawTop` `roads: false`, and its API is `window.AWXMapBase` so it never replaces the radar card's `WXVMap` copy in `site/radar/`), loaded only when the Map tab first opens; it draws water, state and country lines and state names (placed around the airport labels). Until the first tile arrives, or if the tiles can't be reached, the base is drawn from bundled public-domain Natural Earth outlines: `site/map/us.json` (1:50m states and Puerto Rico, outer rings simplified at 0.04°, 52 KB) over the 1:110m land in `site/data/map-land.json`; the footnote then says the detailed map couldn't load. "© OpenStreetMap" shows while the vector map is drawn.
- **Accessibility.** The canvases are hidden from assistive technology; the list under the map ("Airports on the map", reached by scrolling or the List button) mirrors the dots exactly: same filter, same hour, worst first, each row a button that opens the sheet, with the level word and headline. The slider's `aria-valuetext` reads the hour label. The buttons, segments and slider are at least 44 px tall.
- **Checks.** `window.AWXMap._state()` (dots, list rows, base kind, offset, levels and headlines per airport), `pos(code)`, `setOffset(k)`, `setFilter(k)`. The check page (`site/map/check.js`, mock mode, `?test=thunderstorm-ground-stop` in a hidden 390 px frame) requires the tab to render, one dot and one list row per airport, a tap on ORD's dot to open its sheet, the slider to recolour dots, every dot to match `AWXApp.outlook` at that hour, and no errors. The Navigation group also checks the map's dots and list.

The map does not imply worldwide forecast coverage: airports outside status.json carry weather forecasts only, as the footnote says.

**Quiet traveler flow.** Normal airport cards and sheets omit routine baseline delay commentary. A higher future severity is labeled Upcoming on the airport card; Now and Coming up retain their own conditions and windows. Map colours and the list describe the selected hour, while tapping explicitly opens current airport status.

### Accuracy safeguards

BTS arrival dates use scheduled elapsed minutes and both airports’ time zones; westbound clock changes do not imply next-day arrival. Unknown origin zones omit the arrival side rather than inventing a date. The catalog supplies endpoint zones even when only the destination is a training airport. Rebuild BTS aggregates and retrain after this change; older models retain their published validation limits until a replacement passes the safety gate.

Trip weather severity stays visible as **Weather concerns** when a High/Severe weather warning lacks a calibrated likely-delay prediction. An arrival ground stop at the departure airport is informational for outbound flights. Destination programs are checked against exact effective times and explicitly listed departure airports; facility/airline scopes remain unverified and are qualified for the traveler. Airport-wide FAA restrictions do not confirm an individual flight’s assigned departure time.

Delay forecasts identify airport-specific versus pooled model coverage. An airport without training/validation support shows **Delay forecast uncertain** and qualifies a quiet trip. A pooled baseline is never described as that airport’s usual delay rate. Confirmed FAA restrictions and weather warnings remain visible. Training outputs list their airports explicitly; older models use their airport coefficient keys to identify coverage.

National NWS alerts (`poller/nws-wide.mjs`) use published polygons or affected-zone geometry. Alerts with only a whole-county SAME code resolve through the NWS county-zone endpoint and the existing geometry cache. Partial-county, statewide, malformed and unresolved codes remain explicit coverage failures; no alert is silently dropped. SAME format: [NWS documentation](https://www.weather.gov/nwr/sameenz); state codes: [Census table](https://www2.census.gov/geo/docs/reference/state.txt).

My airports also includes scheduled trip airports temporarily on their airport-local departure or arrival day, labeled “In your trip today.” Saved favorites and their stars/order remain unchanged; temporary airports expire with the local day and are never written by reordering. Flight previews are compact airport-outlook cards; verified airline flight status is unavailable.

Airport overview status and Looking ahead headlines carry their severity color without a duplicate category badge. Main-page airport cards retain the category badge. Touch timelines share the same 240 ms hold/horizontal-drag activation in all views, with vertical swipes reserved for scrolling.

Navigation keeps a fixed 52 px footprint on refresh. Startup/layout-driven scroll cannot compact it; sizing animations start with user scrolling. Compact controls have a 40 px visual height and 44 px touch areas, inside a narrower 300 px bar.

## Decoded airport forecasts

Weather and Pilot details show a period-by-period TAF breakdown from NOAA’s structured forecast groups. `poller/taf-periods.mjs` uses the same prevailing-condition helper as risk scoring; display periods preserve exact change times, inherited temporary conditions, probability groups and gradual-change windows. This is display-only and does not change scoring or the delay model. Global weather shards carry the same periods as `tp`. Forecast periods that have ended are omitted; missing decoding stays explicitly unavailable. Wind is shown in mph, ceilings in feet above the airport, and visibility keeps “more than” limits. Traveler probability labels use words; Aviation keeps percentages and flight categories.

The information buttons explain METAR observations and TAF forecasts using [NOAA Aviation Weather Center definitions](https://aviationweather.gov/help/data/). Raw reports remain in Pilot details; the existing METAR breakdown and LAMP guidance table are preserved.
