# Airports

A static page showing weather and delay risk at ~32 major US airports: big airport codes, colored status pills and a rolling 36-hour risk timeline per airport, with a detail sheet in plain English ("Delays & closures", "FAA traffic notices" incl. the FAA Command Center operations plan, "Weather warnings", "Thunderstorms", "Storm outlook", "Storm forecast") and an Aviation-mode "Pilot details" section holding the coded material (decoded and raw METAR, raw TAF, LAMP table, SIGMET and CWA text, TCF tops). Risk reasons stay coded in the data (and history); the page maps them to traveler wording ("Ceiling 400 ft" → "Very low clouds", "Gusts 30 kt" → "Wind gusts to 35 mph", "GDP avg 49m" → "Arrival delays ~49 min"). Sections with nothing in them are hidden; a line at the bottom says which sources were checked, or which failed. Dark-first, inspired by iOS design.

Every poll's predictions and observed outcomes are also recorded to the `history` branch (see "History") so a later phase can verify and train a model.

## Product goal

A user should feel confident they're aware of any potential disruption, weather or not. Clean by default (traveler language), detail behind toggles (aviation mode), and never silently wrong (a source failure or stale data is always stated).

## Architecture

aviationweather.gov does not send CORS headers, so a browser can't call it. Instead:

1. A GitHub Actions workflow (`.github/workflows/poll.yml`) runs every 10 minutes (and on push / manual dispatch).
2. `poller/poll.mjs` fetches every source server-side (each independent, 20 s timeout, per-source `{ok, at, error}`), scores risk with `poller/risk.mjs`, and writes `site/data/status.json`.
3. The workflow uploads `site/` as a Pages artifact and deploys it. Nothing is committed to `main`.
4. After the deploy, `poller/record.mjs` appends to a checkout of the `history` branch and `poller/history.sh` commits and pushes it (see "History"). Those steps are `continue-on-error`, run after the deploy, and can't fail the job.
5. The page (`site/index.html`, `site/app.js`) only reads `./data/status.json` (re-fetched every 2 minutes and when the tab becomes visible). If it 404s it falls back to the committed `site/data/sample.json` and shows a "Sample data" banner.

The poller exits 0 unless every source failed, so a partial outage still deploys; the page lists failed sources ("FAA delay info unavailable").

A Cloudflare Worker ("Live relay" below) can add fresher data on top: when `data/config.json` names it, the page asks it for its starred and visible airports on every open, refresh and every 2 minutes.

## Data sources

- METAR: https://aviationweather.gov/api/data/metar (plus one `&hours=24` request for the major airports: the timeline's observed past hours, `observed[]`; not a status source, so if it fails `observed` is simply left out and those hours show grey)
- TAF: https://aviationweather.gov/api/data/taf
- Convective SIGMETs: https://aviationweather.gov/api/data/airsigmet (point-in-polygon, currently valid CONVECTIVE only)
- FAA NAS status (ground stops, ground delay programs, delays, closures): https://nasstatus.faa.gov/api/airport-status-information. Closure reasons are NOTAM text; `poller/notam.mjs` reads the scope (full airport / particular runways / limited to some users such as GA), the effective times (`YYMMDDHHMM-YYMMDDHHMM`, `PERM`, `EST`; preferred over the Reopen field) and writes a plain-English summary.
- FAA ATCSCC: https://www.fly.faa.gov/adv/advADB.jsp is "The Most Recent ATCSCC Advisory" (a single advisory, not a list). Usually that is the Command Center's **operations plan** ("ATCSCC ADVZY 072 DCC 10/03/2026 OPERATIONS PLAN", body after "RAW TEXT:" in a `<pre>`), parsed by `poller/opsplan.mjs` (see "FAA operations plan"). When it is a program advisory (ground stop, GDP, AFP with "IMPACTING CONDITION"), it is parsed as before; program advisories linked from the page are followed too (up to 40, 4 at a time, 45 s budget). Per control element and program type the latest advisory decides; it is active when it isn't a cancellation (CNX), its period has started and its end is after now.
- NWS alerts per airport: https://api.weather.gov/alerts/active?point=lat,lon
- SPC Day 1 categorical outlook: https://www.spc.noaa.gov/products/outlook/day1otlk_cat.nolyr.geojson
- NWS LAMP text bulletin (hourly guidance): `https://nomads.ncep.noaa.gov/pub/data/nccf/com/lmp/prod/lmp.YYYYMMDD/lmp.tHH30z.lavtxt.ascii`, newest HH:30 cycle, stepping back up to 3 hours on 404. Real CONUS blocks have hourly P01/PC1/LP1/LC1/CP1/CC1 rows plus PPO, PCO, P06, POZ, POS, TYP, CLD, CIG, CCG, VIS, CVS, OBV, WGS; there is no LP2/CP2 row, and some blocks (e.g. Hawaii) have no probability rows at all. Per airport and hour: WGS gust (kt, "NG" = 0), `tstmProb` = LP1 (1-hour lightning probability for the hour ending at that column; LP2, 2-hour, only if LP1 is missing), `convProb` = CP1 (1-hour convection probability; CP2 fallback), `probHrs` (1 or 2), CIG and VIS categories, TYP, POZ, PPO. Columns are found by character position under the UTC row (right-justified 3-character fields), so sparse rows such as P06 (a value every 6th column) line up.
- AWC TFM Convective Forecast: https://aviationweather.gov/api/data/tcf?format=geojson (Mar–Oct; empty/204 is normal). Live properties: `validTime`/`issueTime` as "YYYYMMDD_HHMM" (UTC), `coverage` ("sparse" → low; "medium"; "high"/"solid" → high), `confidence`, `tops` ("390", ">400"). Areas containing the airport (or within 10 NM of the edge), with valid time, coverage, confidence and tops.
- AWC Center Weather Advisories: https://aviationweather.gov/api/data/cwa?format=json. Live records: `hazard` ("TS"), `qualifier`, `validTimeFrom`/`validTimeTo` (epoch seconds), `coords` [{lat, lon} as strings], `rawText`. Advisories whose polygon contains the airport or passes within 10 NM of it.

- Aircraft movement (ADS-B, see "Movement"): [adsb.fi](https://adsb.fi) open data (primary), [ADSB.lol](https://adsb.lol) (fallback; data © ADSB.lol contributors, [ODbL 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/)).

Requests send `User-Agent: airport-wx (github.com/tylerbridges/airport-wx)`, time out after 20 s, and fail independently; a failed source is listed in `sources` and never stops the poll.

LAMP, ATCSCC, TCF and CWA were first written from documentation; on Oct 3 2026 they were checked against real responses from `raw/latest/` on the `history` branch (LAMP: LP1/CP1 instead of LP2; ATCSCC: the page is the operations plan; TCF: "YYYYMMDD_HHMM" times; CWA matched). The fixtures `lamp.txt`, `atcscc.html`, `tcf.json` and `cwa.json` are those real formats, with only their times turned into template tokens. Parsers return empty results on anything unexpected.

Convective SIGMETs, CWAs and TCF areas count an airport as affected when it is inside the polygon, on its edge, or within 10 NM of it (`NEAR_NM` in `poller/lib.mjs`): SIGMET 80E on Oct 3 2026 ("FROM 30NNW CLT-20N CLT-CLT-30NNW SPA") had CLT as a vertex and plain ray casting missed it.

### FAA operations plan

`poller/opsplan.mjs` reads the DCC operations plan: advisory number, date, issue time (signature line), EVENT TIME, the narrative paragraph between the rules of underscores, and these sections (headers are lines ending in ":"):

- STAFFING TRIGGER(S): "UNTIL 0100 -BNA OPERATIONS" → {facility, detail, until}. "UNTIL hhmm" (UTC) rolls past midnight relative to the issue time.
- TERMINAL CONSTRAINTS: "CLT/ATL/MCO/TPA/IAH/HOU - VCTS", "N90 - WIND" → per airport {reason}.
- TERMINAL ACTIVE / PLANNED: "UNTIL 0059 -SAN GROUND DELAY PROGRAM", "UNTIL 2300 -MCO/TPA GROUND STOP POSSIBLE", "… GROUND STOP/DELAY PROGRAM POSSIBLE" → {airports, program: GS|GDP|GS/GDP, status: active|possible, until}.
- RUNWAY/EQUIPMENT/…(SIRs): "DEN - RWY 16R/34L CLOSED UNTIL 11/05/2026 0000Z" (2- or 4-digit years) → {airport, item, status: closed|limited|out of service|construction|maintenance, runways, until}.
- EN ROUTE CONSTRAINTS/ACTIVE/PLANNED, CDRS/SWAP/…, AIRSPACE FLOW PROGRAM(S) ACTIVE/PLANNED, PLANNED LAUNCH/REENTRY (name, site, primary and backup windows): national lists.
- Narrative sentences with DELAY/DEVIATION name airports ("ZJX REPORTS THAT TPA AND MCO ARE STILL EXPERIENCING SOME DEVIATIONS …, AND DELAYS WILL CONTINUE").

Facility → airports (`FACILITY_AIRPORTS`): N90 → JFK/LGA/EWR; A80 → ATL; C90 → ORD/MDW; NCT → SFO/OAK/SJC; SCT → LAX/SAN/SNA/BUR/ONT/LGB; D10 → DFW/DAL; I90 → IAH/HOU; PCT → DCA/IAD/BWI; A90 → BOS; D01 → DEN; L30 → LAS; P50 → PHX; M98 → MSP; S46 → SEA; F11 → MCO; A11 → ANC; HCF → HNL; in staffing triggers MIA (TRACON) → MIA/FLL. Any other 3-letter code is the airport itself (BNA, PHL, CLT). Centers (ZNY, ZJX, ZOA …) map to no airport and go to the national `opsplan` (for a future national summary).

### Program causes

FAA NAS status and ATCSCC programs get a cause class from their reason text (`poller/cause.mjs`): weather, volume, equipment (outages, radar/ILS, frequencies), staffing (incl. ATC zero), runway (construction, closures, configuration), security, airline (company/airline request, IT outage), vip (VIP movement, TFR), space (launch/reentry), other, unknown. The category before the first "/" or ":" wins, else keywords. Programs score by program type alone; the cause only names the reason, e.g. "Ground stop — air traffic control staffing, until 7:30 PM ET" or "Ground stop — airline request (IT outage)".

## Risk levels

0 None (green "Clear"), 1 Low (teal "Minor"), 2 Moderate (amber), 3 High (orange-red), 4 Severe (purple). The highest matching rule wins. Program rules apply whatever the cause.

- **Severe (4):** FAA ground stop or full airport closure (`AD AP CLSD` with no "TO …"/"EXC"/"PPR" qualifier, all runways closed, or a non-NOTAM closure); active ATCSCC ground stop (advisory or ops plan "TERMINAL ACTIVE") not already in the NAS status; thunderstorm with `+` or gusts >= 45 kt; freezing rain; SPC MDT/HIGH; NWS Tornado, Blizzard, Ice Storm, Hurricane, Extreme Wind warnings.
- **High (3):** ground delay program (NAS status, or an active ATCSCC advisory / ops plan GDP not already listed there); TS or VCTS; LIFR (ceiling BKN/OVC/VV < 500 ft or visibility < 1 sm); gusts >= 35 kt; `+SN`, or SN with visibility <= 1/2 sm; FZDZ or PL; SPC ENH; LAMP thunder chance (LP1) >= 40% ("Thunder chance 45% (LAMP)"); TCF high coverage over the airport; Severe Thunderstorm, Winter Storm, Tropical Storm, High Wind warnings; convective SIGMET over (or within 10 NM of) the airport.
- **Moderate (2):** any general arrival/departure delay; IFR (ceiling < 1000 ft or visibility < 3 sm); gusts >= 25 kt; SN; SPC SLGT; LAMP thunder chance (LP1) 20–39%; LAMP convection (CP1) >= 50% when the thunder chance is lower ("Storms likely nearby (LAMP)"); TCF medium coverage; Center Weather Advisory for thunderstorms/convection or IFR over the airport; Winter Weather, Wind, Dense Fog advisories; ops plan "GROUND STOP POSSIBLE" / "DELAY PROGRAM POSSIBLE" until its time ("FAA plans a possible ground stop until 7 PM (storms)", cause from the airport's terminal constraint, else "conditions"); ops plan staffing trigger at the airport or its TRACON until its time ("Air traffic control staffing shortage until 8 PM — delays possible"); ops plan narrative naming the airport with delays/deviations, until the plan's valid end ("FAA reports delays at MCO/TPA expected to continue").
- **Low (1):** MVFR; RA/DZ/BR; SPC MRGL; a single-runway closure ("Runway 7L/25R closed"); an ops plan terminal constraint with no possible/active program ("FAA reports nearby storms affecting arrivals"); an ops plan SIR runway closure or construction ("Runway 16R/34L closed until Nov 4", the airport's local date; never above Low, and not repeated when the NAS status already lists that runway); a SIR glideslope/ILS outage or limited operations, only in hours whose flight category is IFR or LIFR.
- **Informational (0, shown in the sheet, never sets a level):** SPC TSTM ("General thunderstorms possible in the area (no severe risk)"); closures limited to some users (e.g. "CLSD TO NON SKED TRANSIENT GA … EXC 24HR PPR": "Closed to private (non-scheduled, general aviation) flights unless approved 24 hours ahead. Airline flights aren't affected."; size-limited "CLSD TO NON SKED ACFT WINGSPAN MORE THAN 214FT …": "Closed to very large non-scheduled aircraft (747-8/A380 size). Airline flights aren't affected."; "general aviation" only when GA/TRANSIENT/PRIVATE is in the text); closures that haven't started or have ended; SIR glideslope/limited-ops in VFR/MVFR hours; taxiway SIRs; an ops plan program already in the NAS status (NAS wins; if NAS gives no end, the plan's end is used).

Hourly (24 rows from the top of the current hour): the prevailing TAF state per hour from the base group plus FM/BECMG groups (BECMG takes effect at `timeBec` if present, else `timeFrom`); TEMPO groups count at full level; PROB groups count one level lower with "Chance of" in the reason. Hour 0 takes the METAR (the observation wins: TAF conditions are left out of hour 0 when there is a current METAR) and SIGMETs. FAA programs (ground stop, GDP, delays) and active ATCSCC GS/GDP score every hour until their stated end; with no end they hold 3 hours (5 when the delays are increasing) and say "until further notice". Closures and SIR runway closures score hour 0. NWS alerts and CWAs apply to hours between their start and end; the SPC category applies to hours until 12Z next day; each LAMP LP1/CP1 value applies to the hour ending at its column time (LP2/CP2: 2 hours); a TCF area applies within an hour of its valid time. Ranges are written "4–7 PM" only within one day and one AM/PM half, else "11 AM – 2 PM" or "11 PM – 1 AM tomorrow". `now` = hour 0; `peak` = max over 24 hours (earliest hour of that max). Reasons in a box are deduped, with one Visibility/Ceiling/Gusts item each (the highest; the observed one on a tie). Airports are sorted by peak, then now, then IATA code.

The sheet shows one box ("Minor · through 3 AM") while the level holds; a separate "Peak 4–7 PM" box only when the peak is later and higher than now. The card's line uses the same rule.

## The page (build 2b)

- **Settings** live in `site/prefs.js` (classic script in `<head>`, `window.AWXPrefs`; `require()`-able in Node): `getPrefs()`, `setPref(key, value)`, `onPrefs(fn)` (returns an unsubscribe; also fires for changes made in another tab), `DEFAULTS`, `CATEGORIES`. Stored in localStorage `awx-settings`: `mode` traveler|aviation, `show` {category: bool}, `theme` auto|light|dark (`data-theme` on `:root`; applied before first paint by a small inline script), `clock` 12|24, `codes` iata|icao, `timeRef` airport|mine. The only settings UI is the navigation shell's `site/settings.js` (menu → Settings); its disruption switches take their keys from `CATEGORIES` and labels from `AWXCats.LABELS`.
- **Disruption categories** (`site/cats.js`, pure; `tools/cats.test.mjs`): thunderstorms, winter weather, wind, low clouds & fog, heat, FAA delay programs, ATC staffing & equipment, runway closures, VIP / security, space launches, general thunderstorm info. Each reason string is read back to {category, level, source, confidence} from its text and cause wording; a hidden category drops its reasons, programs, alerts and notices, and each hour's level is recomputed from what is left (a kept reason it can't read keeps the poller's level). Ground stops and full airport closures are never hidden. The test checks every hour of the sample and scenarios reads back to the poller's level.
- **Modes.** Traveler shows no aviation codes outside Pilot details (the check page scans every sheet). Aviation adds flight category, ceiling ft, visibility sm, wind kt and Zulu time to the cards, a Current weather card with LIFR/IFR/MVFR/VFR scales, wind/gust tick bars, the crosswind for the best-aligned runway (`runways` from `data/airports-all.json`, via `AWXExtra.runways`) and the raw METAR with hazard groups highlighted, and shows Pilot details as a static section.
- **Timeline** = a rolling 36-hour window: 12 elapsed hours before the current hour, then 24 forecast hours, keeping Now one-third across even at midnight or a daylight-saving transition. Local-midnight dividers carry Today/Tomorrow labels in the display zone; past hours from `observed` (dimmed; grey when there was no report), the current and later hours from `hours`. A Liquid Glass lens (CSS backdrop-filter, solid fallback) sits on the current hour with "Now · Light rain, low clouds" above it. Card timelines are read-only and allow normal page scrolling; the sheet's 12 px bar (44 px touch surface) scrubs at once (pointer capture, `touch-action: none`), shows each hour live only while pressed and sliding, and springs back to the normal view on release, cancellation, or loss of focus. Taps never keep an hour selected, and there is no Back to now button. "Tomorrow ›" keeps the calendar-day view of tomorrow; "‹ Now" returns to the rolling window. Unavailable forecast hours stay grey with an explicit note. Holding an arrow key previews hour by hour; releasing it returns to the normal view.
- **Sheet:** compact Now / Coming up cards when risk increases later, otherwise one current-status card. Quiet, complete data says "Operating normally"; missing, stale or hidden data qualifies that status. No airport cards expand. Reasons stay brief; operational impact, warnings and relevant traffic notices remain visible below the rolling timeline. Technical/raw data appears as static sections only in Aviation mode. A meaningful forecast delay outlook shows timing and typical duration; routine delay commentary and the duplicate active-delay model card are omitted in Traveler mode. Weather shows conditions, temperature, wind and visibility without repeated explanations. Source health stays visible at the bottom.
- **Home:** a national strip ("U.S.: 1 ground stop, 2 delay programs · storms in the South") from every airport plus the national ops-plan items, opening a list of the affected airports; My airports follows the favourites' order and can be reordered by holding a card for 400 ms (or the "Move up/down" buttons for keyboard and VoiceOver).
- **Sheets** (`site/sheet.js`, `makeSheet(el, {onClose, header, backdrop, scroller, noPull})` → `{opened, closed}`): every sheet (airport, national list, Settings and its pages, search, trip) closes the same ways: drag the header down at any scroll position (pointer events, `touch-action: none` on the header only), pull the content down at scrollTop 0, tap the backdrop, Escape, or the browser/iOS back gesture (a history entry per open sheet; not inside frames). Past 25% of the height or a 0.5 px/ms flick it closes, else it springs back. The page scroll is locked while a sheet is open and restored exactly on close.
- **First screen of the airport sheet** (390×700 checked): airport code, name/location, trips' Your flight when present, compact Now / Coming up (or single status) cards, and the timeline. Held hour previews share a fixed footprint; release restores the normal status.
- **Delay words** (`likelihood()` in `site/delay.js`, tested in `tools/delay-words.test.mjs`): the model score is read through `data/model/report.json`'s test reliability table (what actually happened at each predicted level, interpolated; the raw score if the report is missing). Observed rate < 12% "Delays unlikely"; 12–25% "Usual delays" (within ±25% of the typical rate for the hour) or "Small chance of delays"; 25–45% "Delays possible"; 45–70% "Delays likely"; ≥ 70% "Delays very likely" only when that bin's observed rate was ≥ 70% over ≥ 200 test hours. A bin with fewer than 200 test hours steps down one word; airports whose model skill vs climatology is ≤ 0.02 are capped at "Delays possible"; an FAA ground stop, delay program or reported delays in effect says "Delays happening now" (with the FAA average). Cue "higher/lower than usual" at ≥ 1.25× / ≤ 0.75× typical; size "typically 30–45 min" (median ± 10, rounded to 5). Traveler mode never shows a percentage (the check page scans for one); Aviation mode adds it in brackets. The analog sentence ("about 6 in 10 had delays") appears as visible Aviation-mode text only when it agrees with the words (same band or one apart). Trips use the same words (`tripStatus(…, {words})`).
- **Refresh button**: a full page reload (index.html refetched past the cache first); the 2-minute background refresh is unchanged.
- **Times** follow `timeRef` and `clock` everywhere, including the times written into reason texts (re-resolved from the airport's zone). Zones are shown as EDT/CST/HST etc. (US table by offset and DST, else Intl's short name), never in the timeline.

## Live relay

The Actions build runs every ~5 minutes and the deploy adds a few more, so the page's data is usually 5–15 minutes old. The live relay is a Cloudflare Worker (`worker/worker.mjs`, script name `airport-wx-live`) that answers with data at most about a minute old for the airports the page shows.

- **Endpoints.** `GET /health` → `{ok, version, time}`. `GET /status?ids=ORD,MSP,KFCM` (IATA or ICAO, up to 12; optional `&tz=KFCM:America/Chicago` for airports outside the curated list) → `{live: true, generated, build: {ok, generated}, ids, unknown, sources, airports, wx, h0}`: `airports` are curated airports in exactly the status.json shape (fields the relay doesn't compute carry over from the build), `wx` holds other airports by ICAO in the `data/wx/<letter>.json` shard-entry shape (3-letter codes outside the curated list go to `unknown`), `sources` has every status.json source with `{ok, at, error}` plus `live: true` (fetched by the relay), `from: "build"`, or `stale: true` + `liveError` (the live request failed and the build's value is used).
- **What is live.** Per request: AWC METAR and TAF for the requested ids only, AWC airsigmet, FAA NAS status, and NWS alerts per point for the requested curated airports, each with `User-Agent: airport-wx (github.com/tylerbridges/airport-wx)`. LAMP (~4.5 MB), SPC, TCF, CWA and ATCSCC advisories come from the latest build (`https://tylerbridges.github.io/airport-wx/data/status.json`). Levels are recomputed with the poller's own functions (`poller/core.mjs` → `risk.mjs`), so with the same inputs the result is identical to the build (a test checks this).
- **Failures.** Every source is independent. A failed live source falls back to the build's value and is marked `stale`; for airports outside the curated list the build's shard (`data/wx/<letter>.json`) is fetched only when the live METAR or TAF request failed. 502 only when the build and every live source failed.
- **Caching.** Each upstream response is kept 60 s (AWC asks for at most one request per minute per thread), status.json and shards 120 s, and the finished reply 30 s keyed by the sorted ids. Two layers: memory in the Worker isolate, then the Cache API. On `*.workers.dev` the Cache API does nothing (it needs a custom domain), so there it's per isolate: with many users on many isolates AWC can see more than one request a minute.
- **CORS.** `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` only for `https://tylerbridges.github.io` and `http://localhost:*`; GET and OPTIONS.
- **Limits.** Free plan: 100,000 requests a day, 10 ms CPU per request, 50 subrequests per request (a 12-airport request makes at most ~19: METAR, TAF, airsigmet, FAA, 12 NWS points, status.json, shards). Measured in Node 22 on the fixtures (`node worker/dev.mjs bench`, medians, stub cost subtracted): with every cache empty ~4.5 ms for 4 airports and ~8 ms for 12 (most of it the shared risk functions, ~0.3 ms per airport, and parsing the ~220 KB status.json); with the upstreams cached ~1.6 / ~4.6 ms; a cached reply ~0.3 / ~0.7 ms. The 90th percentile of a 12-airport request with empty caches is over 10 ms (garbage collection), and the first request in a new isolate also compiles the modules, so the free plan's limit can occasionally be hit; the paid plan (30 s CPU) removes that.
- **Page.** `site/data/config.json` = `{"liveUrl": "https://airport-wx-live.<subdomain>.workers.dev"}`, written by the "Live relay URL for the page" step of `poll.yml` once the relay's `/health` is ok (committed default `{"liveUrl": null}`: no relay). `site/app.js` (`// live relay`) asks for starred airports first (non-curated ones through `site/searched.js`), then the visible list, at most 12; merges them over the build; shows "Live · 40 s ago", or "Live data unavailable — showing data from N min ago" (amber) when the call fails, and then shows the build only. The refresh button waits for the relay. Test scenarios and sample data never call it. The check page has a "Live relay" row: `/health` ok and `/status?ids=MSP` with a METAR fetched under 3 minutes ago; with no liveUrl it's a warning ("not configured").
- **Deploy.** `.github/workflows/worker.yml` runs on pushes to main that touch `worker/**`, `poller/**` or `airports.json`, and on manual dispatch. Without the secrets it prints "Cloudflare secrets not set; skipping live relay deploy" and succeeds. ## Trips

People who connect their flight calendar (and anyone who adds a trip by hand) see, for each upcoming flight, the specific things that may affect it: in a "Your trips" section at the top of the home list, in a trip sheet, and as a "Your flight" row in each airport's sheet.

**Source.** The flight-tracking app syncs flights into an iPhone calendar; that iCloud calendar is shared as a public calendar, and its link (`webcal://p…-caldav.icloud.com/published/2/…`, fetched as `https://`) is the GitHub Actions secret `FLIGHTY_ICS_URL`. `poll.yml` passes it to the poll step; `worker.yml` adds it to the Worker as a `secret_text` binding of the same name when the secret exists (the Worker doesn't use it yet). Manual trips are stored only on the device (`localStorage` `awx-trips`) and merged with calendar trips in the page.

**Privacy.** The site is public, so `data/trips.json` holds only airports and times: `{generated, configured, ok, error, source, count, flights, trips: [{id, legs: [{from, to, dep, arr}]}]}` (IATA codes, ISO UTC times; `id` = SHA-256 of the event UID salted with the secret link, not reversible). Flight numbers, names, confirmation codes, seats and notes are read only to recognise flights and are never stored or published. The link is never logged or written to a file: fetch errors are generic ("HTTP 404 from the calendar", "timeout after 20s"), any text containing it is masked, and in Actions its `https://` form and path are masked too (`::add-mask::`). The check page fails if `trips.json` has any key, value or pattern beyond codes, times and hashed ids (flight-number pattern, email, free text).

**Pipeline** (`poller/trips-poll.mjs`, three `// trips hook` lines in `poll.mjs`):

1. Each run fetches the calendar once (20 s timeout). No secret: `trips.json` = `{configured: false, trips: []}`. A failed fetch: `{configured: true, ok: false, error}` — the page says the calendar couldn't be read.
2. `poller/trips.mjs` (pure, RFC 5545) unfolds lines and reads VEVENTs (VALARM/VTIMEZONE ignored): DTSTART/DTEND with TZID (Mozilla-style prefixes stripped), `Z` or floating (the airport's own zone, then `X-WR-TIMEZONE`), DURATION when there is no DTEND, VALUE=DATE (all-day: ignored), STATUS:CANCELLED or a "Canceled:" summary (ignored), RRULE (only the first instance), RECURRENCE-ID overrides (skipped).
3. Flight detection is tolerant because the app's exact event format is unverified: a pair of airport codes joined by → -> - – — "to" or ✈ in SUMMARY, LOCATION or DESCRIPTION ("✈ DL 1234 · MSP → ATL", "DL1234 MSP-ATL"); otherwise a flight marker ("Flight", ✈ or a flight number like "DL 1234") plus the departure airport from LOCATION (a code such as "(MSP)", else an airport name or city) and the destination from "to <city or code>" in SUMMARY ("Flight to Atlanta (DL1234)"), "from X to Y", another known code, or the rest of the summary as a place. Codes must be in the airport list (`airports.json` + `site/data/airports-all.json`, so "THE" or "ABC" in notes don't count); a city maps to its main airport (curated first, then scheduled, larger first: "Chicago" → ORD).
4. Departure = DTSTART, arrival = DTEND, each in its own TZID. Consecutive flights where the arrival airport is the next departure airport and the gap is under 8 h form one trip (connections). Trips are kept when the first departure is at most 7 days ahead and the last departure at most 6 h ago.
5. Every trip airport (origin, connections, destination) joins this run's full pipeline even when it isn't one of the curated majors: it is looked up in `airports-all.json` (+ `airports-extra.json`) for ICAO, lat/lon and zone, fetched with FAA, NWS, SPC, LAMP, METAR/TAF like a major, and marked `trip: true` in status.json (the page keeps those off its All / At risk lists unless starred; the check page reports their METARs under "Trips").
6. A redacted structural sample goes to the raw folder (`trips-sample.json`, listed as `trips` in `sources.json`, so it lands in `raw/latest/` on the history branch): calendar and event property names with their parameter names, time shapes (`TZID=America/Chicago:########T######`), counts (events, flights, all-day, cancelled, not flights, no end, how each flight was detected) and the first six events' SUMMARY/LOCATION/DESCRIPTION with every digit turned into `#` and every word that isn't an airport code or a format word ("Flight", "to", "Terminal", "Gate", "Seat", "Confirmation" …) turned into `x`s — e.g. `✈ XX #### · MSP → ATL`. Check it with `git show origin/history:raw/latest/trips-sample.json`.

`FIXTURES_DIR`'s `trips.ics` is read in fixture mode (tokens `{{ics+90 America/Chicago}}` = local time 90 min from now, `{{icsz+90}}` UTC, `{{icsd+1}}` a date); `TRIPS_OUT` changes the output path (default: `trips.json` next to the status file being written). `poller/fixtures/trips.ics` holds MSP→ORD (ORD ground stop), DCA→DEN→SLC (45-minute connection in the DEN winter storm), a flight 26 h out, an all-day "Trip to Atlanta", a cancelled flight and a dentist appointment.

**Concerns** (`poller/trip-risk.mjs`, pure; `site/trip-risk.js` is a byte-identical copy — `cp poller/trip-risk.mjs site/trip-risk.js`; a test fails if they differ). Per leg, from the airports' status.json entries:

- Departure airport around the departure time and arrival airport around the arrival time (hours overlapping ±1 h): the weather level and reasons (FAA program reasons are taken out and reported separately; when a program set the hour's level, the weather part is scored from its own reasons), plus the Phase 3 delay chance when `hours[i].delay` exists (`{p, minutes}`; ignored when absent). A connection is one window from the arrival to the next departure.
- FAA programs that hit this flight: a ground delay program or ground stop at the ARRIVAL airport at the departure time holds the flight at its origin ("ORD ground delay program: flights to ORD are held — your 6:05 PM MSP→ORD departure may wait ~45 min."; the wait is the program's average delay); at the departure airport a ground stop, closure, departure delays, an ops-plan possible ground stop or delay program, a staffing trigger or FAA-reported delays covering the departure; at the arrival airport a closure, arrival delays, staffing or FAA-reported delays covering the arrival (and a program there once the flight is airborne: arrivals slowed). A GDP at the departure airport is Low (it holds flights arriving there, not departures).
- Connections: tight when under 60 min (90 if any airport on the trip is outside the US); flagged when the connecting airport is Moderate or worse around the arrival or has a delay program (Moderate; High with a GDP or High weather; Severe with a ground stop or closure). Under 30 min with nothing going on: a Low note.
- Each concern is one plain sentence with a level; they are ordered by severity (programs, then connections, then weather). Trip status: On track (≤ Minor), Possible delays (Moderate), Delays likely (High), Disruption (Severe), Too early to tell (no flight time inside the 24-hour forecasts yet), Arrived. The top sentence is the most severe concern, or "No weather or FAA issues expected around your flight times."

**Page** (`site/trips.js`, a module; hooks in `app.js` marked `// trips hook`: `render()` → `AWXTrips.render()`, `renderSheet()` → `AWXTrips.decorateSheet(sheet, a)`, `liveQuery()` → trip airports first in the live relay call; `index.html` has `<section id="trips">` and the script tag). "Your trips" (only when there are trips): route with connection codes small, the local departure date and time, the status pill, the top concern and a mini timeline from an hour before departure to an hour after arrival (hourly levels on the ground at each airport, a dotted line in the air). The trip sheet: each leg with its departure and arrival airports' level at those times, connection time, departure vs. arrival impact, every concern, buttons to open each airport's sheet, and "From your flight calendar · updated N min ago" (manual trips: "Added by you", Edit and Delete). Airport sheets of trip airports get a "Your flight" row ("Your 6:05 PM departure to ORD: delays likely — ORD ground delay program") and a plane marker at the flight's hour on the timeline. The nav shell's Trips tab (`site/nav.js` calls `AWXTrips.render(container)`) lists every trip with "Add a trip" and the calendar line, or an empty state. Settings → Trips & flight calendar (`site/settings.js`, lines marked `// trips hook`) shows the calendar status from `AWXTrips.calStatus()` ("Connected · 3 upcoming flights", "Not connected", or that the last fetch failed), the setup steps, and the trips added on this device (`AWXTrips.list()`; each opens its trip sheet with Edit and Delete); the gear next to "Add a trip" opens it (without the nav shell, a Trips sheet in `site/trips.js` does the same). "Add a trip" (`AWXTrips.openAdd()`) takes from, to, an optional connection (with its landing and leaving times), the date and the local departure and arrival times; airports use the search component (`mountSearch`).

**Setup (one time).**

## History

The workflow keeps a `history` branch (orphan; created on the first run) with:

- `truth/YYYY/MM/DD.jsonl`: one line per poll with the observed outcomes: each airport's new METAR (skipped when its obsTime was already recorded), the FAA program state with cause classes, ATCSCC advisories that are active or new, and the FAA operations plan (national `opsplan` and each airport's `opsplan` items) on the first line with a new plan. Sources that failed are listed in `down`.
- `forecast/YYYY/MM/DD.jsonl`: one line per UTC hour (the first poll of the hour) with the predictions: TAF, LAMP, SPC, TCF, CWA, NWS alerts and the 24 hourly risk levels and reasons.
- `raw/latest/`: the first 200 KB of each source's latest raw response, overwritten every poll, plus `sources.json` (ok, error, http status, bytes, url, when captured). This is the place to check live formats.
- `README.md` describing all of it (written on first creation from `poller/record.mjs`).
- `movement/`: ADS-B traffic state, baseline and one log line per run (see "Movement"; `movement/README.md` describes the files).
- `changes/`: the change log's state (`changes/state.json`) and one line per event (`changes/YYYY/MM/DD.jsonl`; see "Change log and morning brief"; `changes/README.md` describes the files).

Lines are compact JSON (nulls/empties dropped, ISO times shortened). Locally: `node poller/poll.mjs --fixtures && node poller/record.mjs /tmp/history` (raw samples go to `.cache/raw`; `--raw <dir>` changes that). `poller/history.sh prepare|push` is the git side the workflow runs; it never creates an orphan branch unless the remote positively has no `history` branch, and on a rejected push rebases once and retries.

Note on size: raw/latest changes every poll, so its old versions accumulate in the branch's git history (a few hundred KB compressed per poll at most).

## Movement

Aircraft data: **[adsb.fi](https://adsb.fi)** (primary) and **[ADSB.lol](https://adsb.lol)** (fallback; © ADSB.lol contributors, ODbL 1.0). Movement figures computed from ADSB.lol data (`movement.json`, the history log) are offered under the same ODbL 1.0 licence.

Sources (checked 2026-10-03; the sandbox couldn't reach the feeds or their websites, only their GitHub docs):

| Feed | Endpoint | Limits | Use allowed | Attribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| adsb.fi ([docs](https://github.com/adsbfi/opendata)) | `https://opendata.adsb.fi/api/v3/lat/{lat}/lon/{lon}/dist/{nm}` (up to 250 NM; the v2 lat/lon/dist form still works but is deprecated and answers in a different format) | 1 request/s on public endpoints; 400/401/403/404/429 replies count toward the limit and repeated invalid requests get a temporary IP block | "personal, non-commercial use only"; no resale/licensing; automated polling not excluded | "You must cite adsb.fi and include a link to our home page" |
| ADSB.lol ([API source](https://github.com/adsblol/api), [docs page](https://github.com/adsblol/website/blob/main/content/en/docs/open-data/api.md)) | `https://api.adsb.lol/v2/point/{lat}/{lon}/{radius}` (radius capped at 250 NM in the source) | "dynamic based on the environment load"; no key today, "in the future, you will require an API key" (by feeding) | "The API is available to everyone"; "You can use the API for free" | ODbL 1.0 (attribution + share-alike for derived databases) |
| airplanes.live | `https://api.airplanes.live/v2/point/{lat}/{lon}/{radius}` | Unverified (its API guide couldn't be reached) | Unverified | Unverified — not used |

Replies are ADSBexchange-v2 style (`ac: [{hex, flight, lat, lon, alt_baro (ft or "ground"), alt_geom, gs, track, track_rate, baro_rate, geom_rate, category, seen, seen_pos}]`, field meanings from [readsb's README-json](https://github.com/wiedehopf/readsb/blob/dev/README-json.md)); the parser also accepts `aircraft: [...]`.

Each poll: one snapshot within 15 NM of each of the 32 airports, sequentially, at least 1.1 s apart per feed, 60 s budget (whatever didn't fit goes first next run; `state.next`); a failed adsb.fi request is retried on ADSB.lol, and after 3 adsb.fi failures in a row ADSB.lol goes first. That is 32 requests per run, ~9,200 a day at one run per 5 minutes. Dropped: non-ICAO (`~`) addresses, surface vehicles and obstacles (category C*), gliders/balloons/UAVs (B*), positions over 60 s old.

Classification against the field (height above field = `alt_baro` + METAR altimeter correction − field elevation; elevation from `airports-all.json` if it gets an elevation column, else the METAR `elev`, else the last known, else 0 ft, which the run log names):
- Ground: "ground", or under 100 ft above the field within 3.5 NM. Taxiing 5–40 kt, stationary under 5, landing/takeoff roll over 40. Ground aircraft farther out belong to another airport and are ignored.
- Departing: within 8 NM, under 4,000 ft, climbing over 500 fpm, track within 90° of the bearing away from the field.
- Arriving: within 12 NM, under 4,000 ft, descending over 300 fpm, track within 90° of the bearing to the field.
- Holding: within 40 NM at 4,000–20,000 ft and turning: the feed's `track_rate` at least 1.5°/s, else a 45°+ heading change since the last snapshot (≤ 15 min old) with under 40% of the expected progress. With 15 NM snapshots only holds close in are seen.
- Takeoff: a hex departing or on the ground (moving, and not just landed) last time that is departing, airborne or gone now. Landing: arriving last time and on the ground or gone now, or first seen on the landing roll. A parked aircraft that disappears (transponder off) is not a takeoff; a go-around isn't a departure.

Rates: each departure/arrival hex is counted once per hour. `depHr`/`arrHr` = unique hexes over the last 60 minutes ÷ coverage (snapshots in that hour ÷ 12, capped at 1). Finished clock hours with coverage ≥ 0.5 become baseline samples per airport × hour of week (local time, 0 = Sunday 00), newest 4 kept, with departures per hub airline. Baseline, first available: our own log (median, at least 3 samples) → BTS scheduled departures for that hour of week, if the Phase 3 output in `site/data/model/` has them (`schedule.json`, or a `sched`/`schedule` object `{IATA: {dep: [168 or 24], arr}}` in `model.json`/`fallback.json`; none today) → none ("Learning normal traffic (N of 21 days)"). `index` = depHr ÷ baseline depHr (null under 3 departures/hr normal).

`site/data/movement.json` (git-ignored, written every run): `{v, generated, run: {ok, airports, of, ms, outOfTime, src, errors, failed}, sources, learning: {since, days, of}, airports: {IATA: {depHr, arrHr, taxiOut, holding, asOf, coverage, n, raw: {dep, arr}, baseline: {depHr, arrHr, n, source: "own"|"bts"|null}, index, sentence, stale?, elev?}}, airlineAlerts: [{airline, name, hubs, sentence}]}`. Sentences: "Departures 38/hr vs 61 normal (↓38%)" when the index is under 0.7 or over 1.3, "Moving normally", "Limited data this hour (5 of 12 checks)" under 0.6 coverage, "Learning normal traffic (N of 21 days)".

Airline alerts: a carrier (`AIRLINES` in `poller/movement.mjs`: majors with hubs, plus regional and cargo codes) whose departures were under 30% of its own baseline at 3+ of its hubs in each of the last 2 finished hours (coverage ≥ 0.6, its baseline ≥ 3/hr there).

Page (`site/movement.js`, `window.AWXMovement`): the card line "Departures running 38% below normal" (only with an index under 0.7 or over 1.3, coverage ≥ 0.6, a baseline and data under 20 minutes old); the sheet card "Traffic right now" (departures and arrivals per hour as bars with a tick at normal, taxiing out, holding nearby, as-of time, source line "From community ADS-B receivers (adsb.fi) · baseline: our log, 12 days"); airline alerts as a banner above the list (or in `#natstrip` when that exists). `SOURCES` is exported for Settings → Data & checks; `check.html` shows a "Movement feed" row (warns, never fails).

History (`movement/` on the `history` branch): the workflow copies `state.json`/`baseline.json` to `.cache/movement/` before the poll and `node poller/movement.mjs record history/` writes them back with one log line per run (`movement/YYYY/MM/DD.jsonl`: `{t, src, skip?, airports: {IATA: {dep, arr, taxi, ground, holding, coverage, byAirline}}}`, dep/arr = first counted that run). Both steps are `continue-on-error`. Fixtures: `poller/fixtures/movement/` (ORD snapshots 5 minutes apart, moved to every airport, plus `seed.json` with a seeded history: ORD below normal, LAS above, ANC/HNL learning, a United alert).

## Change log and morning brief

**Change log** (`poller/changes.mjs`, `poller/changes.test.mjs`; steps marked `brief hook` in `poll.yml`). Each run compares the new status.json (and movement.json) with the previous run's state (`changes/state.json` on the history branch, copied to `.cache/changes/` before the poll the way movement's state is) and emits per-airport events `{t, iata, kind, from, to, sentence, cause?, prog?}`: risk level up/down ("Risk up to High (storms)"), FAA program started / ended / extended (ground stop, delay program, FAA delays; NAS status or an active ATCSCC GS/GDP: "Ground stop started (storms)", "Ground stop lifted", "Ground stop extended until 5:30 PM"), full airport closure start/end, a new NWS warning ("Severe Thunderstorm Warning issued"), the near-term delay word changing ("Delays possible → Delays likely"; words from `site/delay.js` `likelihood()` for the current and next two hours, logged only when one side is "Delays likely" or stronger and never to/from "Delays happening now"), the ops plan adding or dropping a possible ground stop, and departures moving below/above/back to normal (movement.json, only fresh entries with coverage ≥ 0.6 and a baseline). Level, word and movement changes are debounced: one that reverts within 10 minutes is ignored, and a confirmed one is timed when first seen. A source that failed (FAA, ATCSCC, NWS, or no national ops plan) keeps its previous state, so an outage never reads as "lifted". Sentences carry no time of their own (the page prefixes it). Output: `site/data/changes.json` = `{v, generated, since, events}` (last 36 h, newest first; deployed with the page, git-ignored; `{error, events: []}` when the step failed) and, via `node poller/changes.mjs record history/`, `changes/YYYY/MM/DD.jsonl` (UTC day of the event; a line already there isn't added again). Locally: `node poller/poll.mjs --fixtures && node poller/changes.mjs --fixtures` (a simulated quiet previous state, or `poller/fixtures/changes-prev.json`). Scenario builds write `site/data/scenarios/<name>/changes.json` the same way; a scenario's `changes-prev.json` (template tokens allowed) overrides airports' previous state (`{at, airports: {IATA: {lvl: {v}, faa: {type: {end, cause}}, adv, closed, warn, plan, word, mv}}, recent: [events]}`) — `recovering` uses it for the lifted EWR ground stop.

**Morning brief** (`site/brief.js`; hooks marked `brief hook` in `app.js`, `index.html`, `nav.js`, `navui.js`, `testmode.js`, `check.js`, `check-scenarios.js`). A compact glass card at the top of the Airports tab, shown 4–11 AM device time and otherwise from Menu → Today's brief; Dismiss hides it until the next device-local day (localStorage `awx-brief`, try/catch). For the starred airports plus today's trip airports: one headline ("All clear at your 5 airports today" / "2 airports need attention": worst level today Moderate+, an active ground stop/closure/delay program, or a notable "Delays likely" or stronger), up to three lines (today's trips with their status first, then each airport and its worst period today: "ORD: Delays likely 4–8 PM — storms", "EWR: Ground stop until 5:45 PM — storms", "EWR: Delays happening now — ground stop lifted 5 PM"), one national line when there are closures, ground stops, delay programs or regional storms, and a muted note when data is missing, stale or hidden by Settings. It reads the app's filtered view (hidden categories don't count; ground stops always do), the time settings, trips (`AWXTrips._state()`) and changes.json, and re-renders with every data refresh. The airport sheet's **Today** card (below the timeline and delay outlook) lists that airport's events since local midnight in the display zone, newest first, six at most, then "+N earlier" as plain text; it appears only when there is an event, and hidden categories' events are left out. Check page: a "Change log" row (live: loads, well-formed, built under 20 min ago; every scenario: well-formed) and the assertions `change`, `brief`, `today`.

## Backtest (baseline accuracy)

`.github/workflows/backtest.yml` (manual: Actions -> "Backtest baseline" -> Run workflow; inputs `months`, default 2, and `airports`, default `all`) replays history through the TAF rules in `poller/risk.mjs`:

- Period: the latest `months` months with a published BTS On-Time zip (current month and the 5 before it are probed); if none is found, the last complete months, weather only.
- Forecasts: historical TAFs from IEM (`cgi-bin/request/taf.py`), parsed by `poller/taf-parse.mjs` into AWC JSON shape and scored per hour with `tafHour`, at lead buckets 0-3, 3-6, 6-12 and 12-24 h (latest TAF issued at or before H - lead). TAF rules only: no FAA/NWS/SPC/SIGMET history.
- Truth: IEM METAR/SPECI (`asos.py`) scored with the same `assessConditions` thresholds (max over the hour), and BTS departures per local scheduled hour (disrupted = >= 20% of departures 15+ min late with weather/NAS delay, or >= 5% weather cancellations; >= 5 departures).
- Output: `reports/baseline-YYYY-MM-DD.md` + `.json` and `reports/samples/` (first 50 KB of the raw IEM TAF/METAR CSVs and the BTS header) committed to the `history` branch and uploaded as an artifact. POD/FAR/CSI/bias per level threshold and phenomenon, confusion matrices, disruption reliability table, persistence and climatology baselines.

Phase 3 fixed the IEM row-per-group TAF rebuild: validity is now 30 h from the issue hour (or the latest group end if later). Before, it ended at the last TEMPO/PROB group's end, so later hours of such TAFs went unscored (the Oct 3 2026 baseline was made with that bug).

Locally: `node tools/backtest.mjs --fixtures` (reads `tools/fixtures/`, writes to the system temp dir unless `--out`). `node poller/run-tests.mjs` runs all unit tests, including `tools/`.

## Delay model

The risk level says *what* weather is coming; the delay model answers "is this likely to cause a real delay?" with a calibrated chance per airport and hour, the usual chance for that hour, the expected delay length and a plain historical comparison ("In 214 similar evening hours at ORD since Aug 2024, 131 (61%) had delays of 15+ min; median 38 min."). The risk levels are unchanged; `delay` sits next to them in every hour.

**Real delay hour (the target).** In that local hour at the airport, at least 25% of scheduled departures *or* of scheduled arrivals were 15+ min late with BTS `WeatherDelay` or `NASDelay` minutes, or at least 5% of them were cancelled for weather/NAS (`CancellationCode` B or C). A side (departures by Origin + CRSDepTime, arrivals by Dest + CRSArrTime, next day when the arrival clock is earlier than the departure clock) counts only with >= 5 flights; hours where neither side has 5 are skipped. Also kept per hour: flights, shares, median and 90th-percentile minutes of late flights, mean delay. Caveat: BTS gives causes only for flights *arriving* 15+ min late, and NAS includes volume/equipment as well as weather.

**Data** (`tools/train-data.mjs`). The last 24 months with BTS published (two winters). Per airport and lead bucket (0-3, 3-6, 6-12, 12-24 h; the latest TAF issued at or before H - lead, at most 30 h old) the IEM TAF (`taf.py`, row-per-group CSV, parsed by `poller/taf-parse.mjs`) is replayed with `tafHourParts`/`tafHour` from `poller/risk.mjs`. IEM requests are <= 1/s with retries/backoff (5–180 s, Retry-After honoured), chunked by 3 months (falling back to single months) and cached in the run; samples of every raw format go to the artifact and `reports/samples-train/`.

**Features** (`poller/delay.mjs` `tafSummary`/`condSummary`/`encode`, shared by training and live scoring): rule level (TAF only) and level x lead; ceiling and visibility categories; thunder (prevailing TS, prevailing VCTS, TEMPO, PROB30/40), heavy thunder, thunder x lead and x time of day; gust bins (15/25/35 kt, PROB-only gusts), wind 20+ kt; crosswind on the best runway (runway headings from `site/data/airports-all.json`, stored in the model) in 10/15/25 kt bins; precip type RA/SN/heavy SN/FZ/PL (prevailing+TEMPO, and PROB-only); TEMPO IFR; hour, weekday, month; airport; lead; the persistence observation (latest METAR at most 3 h before the time the forecast is made: level, thunder, IFR, wintry precip, gusts, crosswind, each x lead); hub cascade (`HUBS`: each airport's main connecting hub, e.g. MSP -> ORD, BOS -> EWR: thunder, PROB thunder, IFR, snow/ice, gusts, level High+ at the hub); and the airport x month x hour climatology as a continuous input. Optional (`--lamp`, off by default): IEM LAMP archive LP1/CP1 (`mos.py?model=LAV`, format unverified; the parser looks for `runtime`, `ftime`, `lp1`/`cp1` columns and logs a sample).

**Model** (`tools/train.mjs` -> `site/data/model/model.json`). L2-regularised logistic regression on the one-hot features with lead as a feature, fitted by IRLS (Newton) in plain JS; the global intercept and the climatology term are unpenalised and per-airport intercepts shrink toward the global one. Lambda (1/10/100) is chosen by out-of-fold log loss over three contiguous time blocks of the training months, and the out-of-fold predictions fit an isotonic calibration (PAV, <= 60 knots). Expected minutes: median late-flight minutes among real-delay hours by airport x predicted-probability decile (global decile when an airport has < 15 hours).

**Validation.** Strict time split: test = the newest 4 months; when none of them is December–February the test block reaches back to the newest winter month (at most 8 months); training = everything before. Reported on the test months: Brier score and skill vs (a) climatology by airport x hour x month and (b) the current rule levels mapped to their historical rates (lead x level, training months); AUC; a 10-bin reliability table (also for the rule mapping); catching real delay hours (model >= 50%/30% vs rule High+/Moderate+); hours where the model said much higher/lower than usual vs what happened; per lead, season and airport; error of the expected minutes. Output: `site/data/model/report.json` (read by `site/accuracy.html`) and `reports/model-YYYY-MM-DD.md/.json` on the history branch.

**Safety gate.** The workflow writes `site/data/model/model.json` only if test Brier skill vs climatology > 0 **and** the model's Brier beats the rule-level mapping (and there are >= 200 test hours). Otherwise the previous model stays and the report says "not deployed" (with the reasons) and which model is live. `fallback.json`, `report.json` and `analogs/` are always updated.

**Fallback.** Without a usable `model.json` (none yet, or built for another feature `SPEC`), the scorer uses `fallback.json`: the TAF rule level -> its historical real-delay rate per lead bucket (computed in every training run from all months), the climatology for "usually X%", and median minutes per level. Until the first training run the committed `fallback.json` is a seed from the June–July 2026 baseline backtest (departures only, >= 20% definition; marked `source: "seed"`; "usual" is the airport's rate then), so the page always has a historically grounded number.

**Historical comparison (analogs).** Every past hour (lead 0-3 h forecast) gets a signature: dominant hazard (storms > snow/ice > low cloud/fog > wind > clear) x severity x time of day (morning 5–11, afternoon 12–16, evening 17–21, overnight) x season. `site/data/model/analogs/<IATA>.json` stores per bucket `[hours, real-delay hours, median minutes, weather/NAS cancellation share]` (buckets with >= 15 hours). The sentence uses the finest bucket with >= 15 hours; coarser buckets drop the time of day (then season) and say "hours with similar conditions".

**FAA overrides.** An FAA ground stop or ground delay program in effect (NAS status, active ATCSCC advisory, or an active ops-plan program) sets p = 1 ("delays happening now") over its hours, held 3 h (5 h when increasing) without a stated end like the risk rules; minutes = the FAA's stated average delay when given (`minutesFrom: "faa"`). A general FAA delay with a range of 15+ min sets p = 1 in hour 0 (minutes = mid-range). An ops-plan "GROUND STOP/DELAY PROGRAM POSSIBLE" raises p to at least the historical rate at which such possible programs became real ones at that airport within their window, computed from the `history` branch's `truth/` log in each training run (`fallback.programs`, needs >= 5 cases), else 0.5 (`rateFrom: history|default`); it never lowers p.

**Live scoring.** `poller/delay.mjs` `scoreHours` is pure ESM: `poll.mjs` loads `site/data/model/` from disk (`loadDelayModel`, env `DELAY_MODEL_DIR` overrides) and `core.mjs` `assemble` adds `hours[].delay` (marked `// phase3 hook`). The live relay fetches `data/model/model.json`, `fallback.json` and the requested airports' analogs from the Pages site (kept 6 h; a 404 is cached too), scores with the same function (hubs it wasn't asked for use the build's TAF), and carries over the build's numbers if the model files can't be fetched.

**Page.** `site/delay.js` (module, `window.AWXDelay`) supplies the shared delay words. Airport details omit routine delay rates; a forecast outlook appears for weather/program risk or a calibrated rate of at least 45% that is higher than usual. It shows the timing, typical duration, and source basis. Aviation mode also shows calibration and analog text without expanders. Normal aircraft movement is omitted in Traveler sheets; unusual movement gets a brief status and update time. `site/accuracy.html` and `site/accuracy.js` explain reliability, comparisons, sample sizes and model performance.

**Retraining.** Actions -> "Train delay model" (`.github/workflows/train.yml`; also monthly on the 22nd). Inputs: `months` (24), `airports` (all), `test_months` (4), `lamp` (false), `publish` (true). Runtime ~2–3.5 h (BTS zips ~30–45 min, IEM ~1.5–2.5 h at 1 request/s, fitting a few minutes); timeout 300 min. Locally: `node tools/train.mjs --fixtures` runs the whole pipeline on a synthetic world (`tools/train-fixtures.mjs`, real file formats; FIXTURE in every output) into the temp dir, or `--out <dir>`; `node tools/train-data.mjs bts|build …` and `node tools/train.mjs --data <dir>` are the real steps.

## Plain English (`poller/plain.mjs`)

Pure ESM with no imports, used by the poller and (as the byte-identical copy `site/plain.js`) by the page. Approach: one source file, committed copy; `poller/plain.test.mjs` fails if the copy drifts (`cp poller/plain.mjs site/plain.js`).

- `plainMetar(metar)`, `plainTafHour(fcstGroup)`: traveler sentences in mph, miles and plain cloud words ("Rain and low clouds", "Thunderstorms with heavy rain", "Gusts to 40 mph", "Fog — visibility under 1 mile"; TEMPO = "At times …", PROB30 = "30% chance of …"). Accepts AWC JSON or the status.json `metar` shape.
- `travelerImpact(level, conditions)`: what it means for flights, worded as possible/likely, never certain ("Arrivals are often slowed in these conditions; delays of 30+ min possible", "Storms can pause departures and arrivals (ground stops) — expect delays", "Strong crosswinds may cause delays or diversions", "De-icing and slower operations — delays likely", "Flights operating normally"). FAA programs in `conditions.faa` take precedence; GA-only (`scope: limited`) closures don't count.
- `plainSigmet(s, {tz})` / `plainCwa(c, {tz})`: "Area of severe thunderstorms moving east at 25 mph, tops to 45,000 ft, until 9 PM" (movement is the direction storms move toward). The CWA text field name is unverified (`cwaText`, `rawText`, `text` or `raw` are tried).
- `plainAlert(alert, {tz, now})`: "Winter Storm Warning until Sun 6 AM", "Wind Advisory, 2–8 PM".
- `aviationLines(metar | fcst)`: pilot lines for aviation mode (flight category, ceiling ft AGL, visibility sm, wind dir/speed/gust kt, decoded weather codes, cloud layers, temp/dew point).

Not yet wired into status.json's own fields (that happens when the app gets aviation mode); `poller/global.mjs` already uses it for searched airports.

`site/outlook.js` evaluates the same airport-hour outlook for the detail sheet and Map. Forecast wording describes airport disruption risk, not the probability that an individual flight will be delayed. Directional FAA impact, reported delay trends, scheduled restriction end times, and the FAA’s published extension outlook appear as brief static rows when available. Forecast improvement is separate from a restriction’s scheduled end. Trip context flags overlap with the highest-risk forecast window. No flight outcome or cancellation feed is implied.

Map shows My airports, All monitored airports, or At risk at Now / +3 / +6 / +12 / +24 hours. Confirmed restrictions, forecast risk, normal operations, and unavailable data have distinct symbols; stale or missing coverage stays grey. Pins cluster and support touch selection, drag, pinch, zoom buttons and keyboard navigation; the airport list is an accessible alternative. Saved trip routes use airport codes only. Geography is a bundled Natural Earth public-domain land outline (`site/data/map-land.json`), with no map service dependency. The map does not imply worldwide forecast coverage.
